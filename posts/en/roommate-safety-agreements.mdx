- **Creates accountability** so everyone takes safety seriously

- **Builds trust** among roommates by showing mutual respect for personal safety

## Essential Components of a Roommate Safety Agreement

### Emergency Contact Information

Start your safety agreement by collecting and sharing comprehensive emergency contact information for all roommates. This should include:

- **Primary emergency contacts** (parents, guardians, siblings)
- **Secondary contacts** (close friends, other family members)
- **Medical providers** and insurance information
- **Campus security numbers** and local police/fire departments
- **Resident advisor or landlord contacts**

With **One Tap Alert's Secure Vault** feature, roommates can store copies of important documents like insurance cards, medical information, and identification in encrypted storage that's accessible during emergencies. This eliminates the scramble to find critical information when every second counts.

### Door and Window Security Protocols

Physical security forms the foundation of any safety agreement. Establish clear rules about:

- **Always locking doors** when leaving the apartment or going to sleep
- **Window security** on ground-floor units
- **Key management** and who has copies
- **What to do if keys are lost** or if someone gets locked out
- **Security system codes** (if applicable) and who can share them

Consider implementing a buddy system where roommates use **One Tap Alert's live location sharing** when heading home late at night, so others know when to expect them and can verify they arrived safely.

### Guest and Visitor Policies

Disagreements about guests are among the most common roommate conflicts, but they're also critical safety considerations. Your agreement should address:

- **Advance notice requirements** for overnight guests
- **Maximum number of guests** allowed at once
- **Guest behavior expectations** and quiet hours
- **Unknown visitor protocols** (never opening the door for strangers)
- **What to do if a guest makes someone uncomfortable**

Establish that all roommates have the right to feel safe in their home, and any concerns about guests should be taken seriously and addressed immediately.

### Night Out Communication Standards

When roommates go out—whether to parties, dates, or late study sessions—communication can be life-saving. Create standards for:

- **Sharing general plans** (where you're going, expected return time)
- **Check-in expectations** for late nights
- **Emergency protocols** if someone doesn't return as planned
- **Ride safety** (never accepting rides from strangers, using verified rideshare apps)

The **One Tap Alert safety timer** is perfect for these situations. Roommates can set a timer before heading out to a party or late-night study session, and if they don't check in by the designated time, all their emergency contacts—including roommates—are automatically alerted with their live location.

### Alcohol and Substance Safety Protocols

Regardless of personal choices about alcohol or substances, your safety agreement should include protocols for:

- **Never leaving an intoxicated roommate alone**
- **When to call for medical help** without hesitation
- **Protecting roommates from making unsafe decisions** while impaired
- **Designated sober friend responsibilities** during parties
- **Zero tolerance for driving under the influence**

Make it clear that safety always comes before worrying about getting in trouble, and establish that all roommates will support each other in making safe choices.

### Personal Safety When Alone

Discuss what safety measures each person should take when home alone:

- **Keeping doors locked** even when home
- **Not advertising on social media** that you're alone
- **Having a personal safety app** readily accessible
- **Knowing escape routes** from your living space
- **Having a safe room or plan** if someone tries to break in

Each roommate should have **One Tap Alert** downloaded and configured with all roommates as emergency contacts, ensuring that anyone can instantly alert the group if they feel threatened or unsafe, even when home alone.

## Creating Your Roommate Safety Agreement: Step-by-Step

### Step 1: Schedule a House Meeting

Set aside dedicated time when all roommates can focus on creating your safety agreement without distractions. This isn't something to rush through during move-in chaos.

### Step 2: Share Personal Safety Concerns

Go around the room and have each person share their specific safety concerns. Someone who's experienced harassment may have different priorities than someone who's worried about medical emergencies. All concerns are valid.

### Step 3: Draft the Agreement Together

Use a shared document where everyone can contribute. Cover all the essential components discussed above, and make sure every roommate has input on each section.

### Step 4: Set Up Technology Solutions

Have everyone download **One Tap Alert** during the meeting and add each other as emergency contacts. Walk through how to use the SOS button and safety timer together so everyone is comfortable with the features before an actual emergency occurs.

Test the system by having one person send a test alert to make sure notifications work properly on everyone's devices.

### Step 5: Post Important Information

Create a physical copy of essential emergency information (numbers for campus security, poison control, nearest hospital) and post it somewhere visible like the refrigerator. While information is stored in **One Tap Alert's Secure Vault**, having a physical backup never hurts.

### Step 6: Review and Update Regularly

Schedule check-ins each semester to review and update your safety agreement. Phone numbers change, roommates move in and out, and what worked in September might need adjustments by January.

## Red Flags and How to Address Them

Even with a solid safety agreement, issues can arise. Watch for these red flags and address them immediately:

### A Roommate Consistently Ignoring Safety Protocols

If someone repeatedly leaves doors unlocked, doesn't communicate when staying out, or dismisses safety concerns, have a direct conversation. If the behavior continues, involve your RA or consider whether the living situation is sustainable.

### Guests Who Make People Uncomfortable

Your home should be a safe space for everyone. If a roommate's guest makes others uncomfortable through comments, behavior, or presence, that guest should not be welcome back—no exceptions.

### Pressure to Engage in Unsafe Behaviors

No one should ever feel pressured to drink excessively, use substances, or participate in risky activities. If a roommate is creating this pressure, it's a serious problem that needs intervention from resident advisors or other campus authorities.

### Dismissal of Safety Concerns

If you express a safety concern and a roommate dismisses or mocks it, this indicates a fundamental incompatibility. Everyone deserves to have their safety concerns taken seriously.

## Special Considerations for Different Living Situations

### Off-Campus Apartments

Off-campus living offers independence but also requires more vigilance:

- **Install additional locks** or security devices if allowed by your lease
- **Get to know neighbors** who can be extra eyes on your unit
- **Understand emergency exits** and fire escape routes
- **Consider renters insurance** that covers theft and liability

### First-Floor or Ground-Level Units

Ground-floor apartments require extra security measures:

- **Keep curtains/blinds closed** at night
- **Add window locks** or security bars if permitted
- **Be extra vigilant** about locking windows
- **Consider motion-sensor lights** outside windows

### Single-Gender vs. Co-Ed Housing

Safety concerns may differ in single-gender versus co-ed living situations, but the fundamentals remain the same: clear communication, mutual respect, and established protocols that everyone follows.

- **Have mental health crisis numbers** saved and posted
- **Agree to check in** if someone seems to be struggling
- **Remove stigma** around asking for help
- **Know campus counseling resources** and how to access them

**One Tap Alert** can be a tool for mental health safety too—if a roommate is having a crisis and needs immediate support, they can alert the group instantly rather than suffering in silence.

## Legal and Liability Considerations

While not legal documents in a formal sense, roommate safety agreements can be important if incidents occur:

- **Document everything** in writing
- **Have all roommates sign** the agreement
- **Keep copies** in a safe place
- **Review your lease** for any safety-related clauses
- **Understand your rights** regarding guests and unwanted visitors

Consult your campus housing office or legal services if you need guidance on creating an agreement that has appropriate protections.
