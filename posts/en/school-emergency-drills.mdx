## Why School Emergency Drills Matter

Emergency drills serve a fundamental purpose: they transform panic into procedure. When a real emergency strikes, the human brain often freezes or makes irrational decisions. Regular drills create neural pathways that allow students and staff to respond automatically, even under extreme stress.

According to the National Fire Protection Association, schools are required to conduct fire drills regularly, but many also practice lockdown, shelter-in-place, and evacuation procedures. These drills:

- **Reduce response time** during actual emergencies
- **Identify weaknesses** in emergency plans before they become critical
- **Build confidence** in students and staff
- **Create accountability** for safety protocols
- **Familiarize everyone** with exits, safe zones, and assembly points

While schools focus on campus safety, parents often wonder how they'll know if something happens. ## Types of School Emergency Drills

Understanding the different types of drills helps families prepare conversations at home and reinforces what students learn at school.

### Fire Drills

The most common and frequently practiced drill, fire drills teach orderly evacuation procedures. Students learn:

- Designated exit routes
- Assembly point locations
- The importance of leaving belongings behind
- How to stay calm and quiet during evacuation
- What to do if the primary exit is blocked

**Pro tip for parents:** Ask your children where their classroom assembly point is located. If they can describe it, they're paying attention during drills.

### Lockdown Drills

Perhaps the most emotionally challenging drill for everyone involved, lockdown drills prepare schools for threats inside or near the building. These drills teach:

- How to quickly secure classroom doors
- Where to hide or position yourself in a room
- The importance of silence and turning off lights
- How to barricade doors if necessary
- When it's safe to respond to authorities

For high school students with smartphones, having **One Tap Alert** installed means they can silently alert their emergency contacts with live location sharing if a real lockdown situation occurs—something that provides parents with critical information without requiring voice calls that could compromise safety.

### Shelter-in-Place Drills

Used for external threats like chemical spills, severe weather, or air quality emergencies, these drills teach students to:

- Move to interior rooms without windows
- Seal doors and vents if necessary
- Remain calm while waiting for all-clear signals
- Follow teacher instructions explicitly

### Evacuation Drills

Different from fire drills, full building evacuations might occur due to bomb threats, gas leaks, or other situations requiring everyone to leave campus entirely. These drills practice:

- Alternative exit strategies
- Off-campus assembly points
- Accountability procedures
- Reunification protocols with parents

During real evacuations, communication becomes critical. ## Best Practices for Parents During School Emergency Drills

As a parent, your role in emergency preparedness extends beyond what happens at school. Here's how to support your children and reinforce drill procedures:

### Have Regular Conversations

Don't wait for drills to happen—proactively discuss emergency procedures with your children:

- Ask what types of drills their school practices
- Listen to their feelings about drills (they can be scary)
- Reinforce that drills are practice to keep everyone safe
- Explain what you would do in various emergency scenarios

### Create a Family Communication Plan

Schools have emergency plans, and your family needs one too. Your plan should include:

- Multiple emergency contacts beyond just parents
- A designated out-of-town contact person
- Reunification procedures if you can't reach the school
- What to do if phones don't work
- Where to meet if home is inaccessible

### Practice Technology Preparedness

For students old enough to have smartphones:

- Ensure they know how to use emergency apps like **One Tap Alert**
- Practice using the SOS button together (most apps have test modes)
- Make sure devices are charged before school

Every school has unique emergency protocols. Make sure you:

- Understand the school's communication system (texts, emails, calls)
- Know where reunification sites are located
- Have current emergency contact information on file
- Understand the difference between drills and real emergencies
- Know the school's policy on students using phones during emergencies

## Common Questions About School Emergency Drills

**How often should schools conduct emergency drills?**

Most states require monthly fire drills and quarterly or semi-annual lockdown and other emergency drills. Check your state and local requirements for specific mandates.

**Should parents tell young children about lockdown drills in advance?**

Yes, but age-appropriately. Focus on "practicing staying safe" rather than specific threats. Reassure them that drills are practice and that teachers will keep them safe.

**Can students opt out of drills?**

Generally no, as drills are considered essential safety education. However, students with trauma histories or anxiety may benefit from advance notice and support from counselors.

**What if my child has a medical condition that makes certain drills difficult?**

Work with the school to develop an individualized emergency plan. Document conditions in the **One Tap Alert** Secure Vault so this information is always accessible if needed.
