## Creating Your Pet Emergency Kit

Every pet owner should maintain a ready-to-go emergency kit that can sustain their pets for at least 72 hours. Here's what to include:

### Essential Supplies

- **Food and Water**: At least three days' worth of pet food in airtight, waterproof containers, plus bottled water
- **Medications**: A week's supply of any prescription medications with clear dosing instructions
- **Medical Records**: Vaccination records, medical history, and veterinarian contact information
- **Identification**: Recent photos, microchip information, and physical descriptions
- **Supplies**: Leashes, collars with ID tags, carriers, litter boxes, waste bags, bowls, and blankets
- **Comfort Items**: Favorite toys or bedding to reduce stress

### First Aid Supplies

Include pet-specific first aid items:
- Gauze pads and bandages

- Emergency contact numbers for your vet and local animal emergency hospitals

## Developing Your Pet Evacuation Plan

A comprehensive evacuation plan answers critical questions before disaster strikes.

### Identify Pet-Friendly Shelters and Hotels

Not all emergency shelters accept pets. Research and document:
- Pet-friendly hotels within 50, 100, and 200 miles of your home
- Animal shelters that provide emergency boarding
- Friends or family members outside your immediate area who could temporarily house your pets
- Veterinary hospitals that offer boarding services

Store this information digitally where you can access it anywhere. Many pet owners use **One Tap Alert's Secure Vault** to keep a document with their complete pet evacuation resource list, ensuring they're never scrambling to find pet-friendly accommodations during a crisis.

### Assign Pet-Specific Emergency Contacts

Designate trusted individuals who can help with your pets during emergencies. These contacts should:
- Have keys to your home
- Know where you keep your pet emergency kit
- Be familiar with your pets' routines, medical needs, and temperaments
- Live close enough to respond quickly

**One Tap Alert** allows you to add unlimited emergency contacts, and you can specify in your emergency plan which contacts are responsible for pet rescue. ### Practice Your Evacuation

Run practice evacuations with your pets every six months. This helps:
- Identify what you can realistically carry
- Reduce pet anxiety by familiarizing them with carriers
- Reveal gaps in your plan
- Build muscle memory for grabbing essential items

Time yourself using **One Tap Alert's Safety Timer** feature during practice runs. Set a countdown for how long you think evacuation should take, and if you exceed that time, you'll know you need to streamline your process.

## Special Considerations for Different Types of Pets

### Dogs

Dogs require leashed control during evacuations. Ensure you have:
- Multiple sturdy leashes (retractable leashes can fail)
- A well-fitted harness and collar with ID tags
- Muzzles if your dog becomes aggressive when stressed
- Recent photos showing distinctive markings

Use **One Tap Alert's Safety Timer** during daily walks. If you have a medical emergency while walking your dog, your emergency contacts will know something's wrong and can come help with both you and your pet.

### Cats

Cats often hide during chaos, making evacuation challenging:
- Keep carriers easily accessible
- Practice carrier training to reduce stress
- Have multiple ways to identify your cat (microchip, collar, photos)
- Include familiar bedding in emergency kits

### Birds

Bird owners face unique challenges:
- Ensure cages or carriers provide adequate protection
- Cover cages to reduce stress during transport
- Maintain proper temperature (many birds are temperature-sensitive)
- Keep dietary needs specific to species

### Small Animals and Reptiles

For rabbits, guinea pigs, hamsters, reptiles, and other small pets:
- Use secure, well-ventilated carriers
- Pack species-specific food (often unavailable at stores)
- Include temperature regulation items (heat packs or cooling packs)
- Bring habitat essentials (water bottles, hiding spots)

Place a pet alert window cling near your front door indicating:
- Number and types of pets
- Where pets are typically located
- Emergency contact information

### Establish Safe Rooms

Identify safe rooms in your home for different emergency types:
- **Tornado/Severe Weather**: Interior rooms without windows, basement areas
- **Fire**: Easy-exit rooms with direct outdoor access
- **Earthquake**: Open areas away from shelves and heavy furniture

### Microchipping and Identification

Microchipping provides permanent identification:
- Ensure contact information is current in the microchip database
- Store microchip numbers in a secure, accessible location

- Remember that microchips require scanners—always use visible ID tags too
