## Recognizing the Signs of a Gas Leak

Before you can respond to a gas leak, you need to recognize one. Natural gas is odorless in its pure form, but utility companies add a distinctive "rotten egg" smell (mercaptan) to help you detect leaks quickly.

### Physical Warning Signs

Watch for these immediate indicators:

- **Sulfur or rotten egg odor** — The most recognizable sign of natural gas
- **Hissing or whistling sounds** — Gas escaping from pipes or appliances
- **Dead or dying vegetation** — Plants near gas lines that suddenly wilt or die
- **Dust or debris blowing** — Unusual air movement near gas lines
- **Bubbles in standing water** — Gas escaping through puddles or water

### Physical Symptoms in People and Pets

Gas exposure can cause immediate health effects:

- Dizziness and lightheadedness
- Nausea or headaches
- Breathing difficulties
- Fatigue and weakness
- Irritated eyes or throat
- Pets acting strangely or trying to leave the area

If you or anyone in your household experiences these symptoms along with a gas smell, treat it as an emergency immediately. ## Immediate Gas Leak Response Steps

When you suspect a gas leak, every second counts. Follow these critical steps in order:

### 1. Evacuate Immediately Without Delay

Don't waste time investigating or trying to locate the source. Get everyone—including pets—out of the building immediately. Use the nearest exit and move at least 100 feet away from the structure.

As you evacuate, use **One Tap Alert's instant SOS button** by pressing and holding for just one second. Your contacts will receive an alert and can see your exact location in real-time, which is crucial if you need to evacuate to an unfamiliar location or if the situation escalates.

### 2. Don't Create Sparks or Flames

During evacuation, avoid these actions that could ignite gas:

- **Don't turn lights on or off** — Electrical switches can create sparks
- **Don't use phones inside** — Wait until you're safely outside
- **Don't start vehicles** — If your car is in an attached garage
- **Don't use flashlights** — Unless they're specifically rated for explosive atmospheres
- **Don't smoke or use lighters**
- **Don't operate any electrical devices or appliances**

Even the smallest spark from a light switch or cell phone can ignite accumulated gas and cause an explosion.

### 3. Leave Doors Open as You Exit

As you evacuate, leave doors open behind you. This helps disperse the gas and reduces the concentration that could lead to an explosion. Don't go back to open windows or additional doors—just leave the ones you pass through open.

### 4. Call for Help from a Safe Distance

Once you're at least 100 feet away from the building (farther if you can still smell gas), call 911 and your gas utility company's emergency line. Provide:

- Your exact address
- Your current safe location
- Description of the gas smell or other symptoms
- Number of people evacuated
- Any visible damage or hissing sounds you noticed

This is where **One Tap Alert's real-time location sharing** becomes invaluable. Your emergency contacts already know where you are and that you're in an emergency, so they can send additional help or meet you at your location if needed.

## After Evacuation: What Happens Next

Once you've evacuated and called for help, follow these important steps:

### Wait for Professional All-Clear

Never re-enter the building until utility company professionals or fire department officials have inspected the property and declared it safe. Even if the smell seems to have dissipated, dangerous levels of gas may still be present.

Professionals will:

- Use specialized detection equipment to measure gas levels
- Locate and repair the source of the leak
- Ventilate the building properly
- Test all gas appliances
- Ensure it's safe to restore gas service

This process may take several hours. Use **One Tap Alert** to keep your emergency contacts updated on your status and location as you wait.

For insurance purposes, document:

- Time you first noticed the gas leak

- Expenses incurred (hotel, meals if displaced)

### Know When to Seek Medical Attention

Even brief gas exposure can cause health effects. Seek medical evaluation if anyone experiences:

- Persistent headaches or dizziness
- Continued nausea or vomiting
- Chest pain or breathing difficulties
- Loss of consciousness (even briefly)
- Confusion or memory problems

## Gas Leak Prevention: Protecting Your Home

The best gas emergency is the one that never happens. Take these preventive measures:

### Regular Inspection and Maintenance

- **Schedule annual inspections** of all gas appliances by certified professionals
- **Check connections** on gas stoves, dryers, water heaters, and furnaces regularly
- **Replace old appliances** that show signs of wear or damage
- **Inspect gas lines** for corrosion, especially in older homes
- **Keep appliances clean** to ensure proper combustion and ventilation

When performing any of these maintenance tasks, activate **One Tap Alert's Safety Timer** to ensure someone knows you're working with potentially dangerous equipment.

### Install Detection Devices

- **Natural gas detectors** that sound an alarm when gas is present
- **Carbon monoxide detectors** on every floor and near sleeping areas
- **Smart detectors** that can alert your phone when you're away from home

Consider adding these devices to your safety plan alongside **One Tap Alert** for comprehensive protection.

### Know Your Shut-Off Valve Location

Every household member should know where the main gas shut-off valve is located and how to turn it off. Take photos of the valve and its location, and store them in **One Tap Alert's Secure Vault** for easy reference.

**Important:** Only shut off gas if you smell gas, hear gas escaping, or are instructed to do so by authorities. Once shut off, only the gas company should turn it back on.

### Create and Practice an Evacuation Plan

Develop a household gas leak evacuation plan that includes:

- Primary and secondary escape routes from each room
- A designated meeting place at least 100 feet from your home
- Responsibilities for helping children, elderly family members, or pets
- Emergency contact numbers memorized or saved in multiple places

Practice this evacuation plan regularly. During your practice drills, use **One Tap Alert's features** to familiarize everyone with how quickly they can alert emergency contacts during a real emergency.

## Common Gas Leak Myths You Shouldn't Believe

Misinformation about gas leaks can be dangerous. Here are myths to ignore:

### Myth 1: "A small gas smell isn't dangerous"

**Reality:** Any amount of natural gas odor indicates a leak. Even small leaks can become large ones quickly, or gas can accumulate in enclosed spaces to explosive levels.

### Myth 2: "I can find and fix the leak myself"

**Reality:** Only licensed professionals should locate and repair gas leaks. Attempting DIY repairs can make the situation worse or cause an explosion.

### Myth 3: "Opening windows will solve the problem"

**Reality:** While ventilation helps, it doesn't eliminate the danger. Evacuate first, then let professionals handle ventilation safely. Operating windows or electrical exhaust fans can create sparks.

### Myth 4: "Gas leaks only happen in old buildings"

**Reality:** New construction can have faulty installations, earthquakes or ground shifts can damage any gas line, and even new appliances can malfunction.

### Myth 5: "My phone is safe to use inside during a gas leak"

**Reality:** Cell phones can create sparks that ignite gas. Wait until you're outside and away from the building before using any electronic device.
