## Pre-Ride Safety Checklist

Before you hit the road or trail, taking time to prepare can make the difference between a fun outing and a potentially dangerous situation.

### Inspect All Bikes Thoroughly

Start every ride with a complete bike inspection for each family member:

- **Brakes**: Test both front and rear brakes to ensure they engage properly
- **Tires**: Check for proper inflation, cracks, or embedded debris
- **Chain**: Ensure it's properly lubricated and tensioned
- **Handlebars and seat**: Verify they're secure and adjusted to the right height
- **Reflectors and lights**: Make sure all visibility equipment is clean and functional

This five-minute check can prevent mechanical failures that might leave your family stranded or cause accidents. Before departing, it's wise to activate **One Tap Alert's safety timer** feature—set it for your expected ride duration, and if you don't check in on time due to a mechanical issue or other emergency, your emergency contacts will automatically be notified with your last known location.

### Ensure Proper Safety Gear for Everyone

Never compromise on protective equipment:

- **Helmets**: Every family member needs a properly fitted, CPSC-certified helmet
- **Bright clothing**: Wear fluorescent colors during the day and reflective materials for dawn/dusk rides
- **Gloves**: Protect hands from falls and improve grip
- **Closed-toe shoes**: Sandals and flip-flops have no place on a bike
- **Eye protection**: Sunglasses or clear glasses shield eyes from debris and insects

For children, consider additional knee and elbow pads, especially for beginners still developing their balance and confidence.

## Planning Your Route for Maximum Safety

Not all bike routes are created equal when you're riding with family. Strategic route planning is essential for a safe and enjoyable experience.

### Choose Family-Friendly Paths

Prioritize routes that offer:

- **Dedicated bike lanes or paths**: Separation from vehicle traffic dramatically reduces risk
- **Low-traffic residential streets**: When bike paths aren't available, quiet neighborhoods are preferable to busy roads
- **Good road conditions**: Avoid routes with potholes, gravel, or poor pavement that could cause falls
- **Rest stops**: Plan for bathrooms, water fountains, and shaded areas, especially with younger children
- **Emergency access points**: Know where you can quickly exit the trail if needed

Before your ride, share your planned route with a trusted contact. With **One Tap Alert**, you can take this a step further by enabling real-time location sharing, allowing your emergency contacts to see exactly where you are throughout your journey without requiring constant check-ins.

### Consider Your Weakest Rider

Family rides move at the pace of your least experienced cyclist. When planning distance and difficulty:

- Start with shorter rides (2-3 miles) for young children
- Choose flat terrain before tackling hills
- Allow extra time—rushing increases accident risk
- Plan for frequent breaks to prevent fatigue

Tired, frustrated children are more likely to make poor decisions on their bikes, so keep rides age-appropriate and enjoyable.

## Communication and Emergency Protocols

Even with perfect preparation, emergencies can happen. Having clear communication plans ensures you can respond quickly when needed.

### Establish Clear Riding Rules

Before departing, review family cycling rules:

- Stay together within sight of each other
- Use hand signals for turns and stops
- Call out hazards ("car back," "pothole," "stopping")
- No headphones or phone use while riding
- Always stop at intersections, even without stop signs

Make sure children understand these aren't optional suggestions—they're safety requirements.

### Prepare for Emergency Scenarios

Discuss what to do if someone:

- Gets separated from the group
- Has a mechanical failure
- Gets injured
- Encounters an aggressive dog or person
- Feels unwell or exhausted

## Teaching Children Bike Safety

Children need age-appropriate safety education to become responsible cyclists.

### Start Young With Basic Skills

Before hitting public paths:

- Practice in a traffic-free area like a parking lot or quiet cul-de-sac
- Teach proper mounting, stopping, and turning
- Ensure they can ride in a straight line without wobbling
- Practice looking behind them without swerving

### Teach Road Awareness

Gradually introduce concepts like:

- Understanding traffic signals and signs
- Making eye contact with drivers
- Anticipating car movements
- Identifying safe places to ride

### Empower Older Children With Safety Tools

As children become more independent cyclists, teach them to use safety technology responsibly. Older children and teens with their own phones should have **One Tap Alert** installed and understand how to use the SOS button if they encounter any problems. This gives them independence while giving you peace of mind.

## Weather and Environmental Considerations

Environmental conditions significantly impact cycling safety.

### Check Weather Before Riding

Avoid cycling in:

- Heavy rain (reduced visibility and traction)
- Strong winds (can blow cyclists off course)
- Extreme heat (risk of heat exhaustion)
- Lightning storms (obvious danger)

If weather changes during your ride, use **One Tap Alert's real-time location sharing** to let your emergency contacts know where you are, then seek shelter immediately.

### Be Aware of Wildlife and Environmental Hazards

Depending on your location, you might encounter:

- Loose dogs
- Snakes on trails
- Bee nests
- Poison ivy or other plants

Teach children to stop and call for adult help rather than trying to handle these situations themselves.

## Post-Ride Practices

Safety doesn't end when you return home.

### Conduct a Post-Ride Debrief

Discuss what went well and what could be improved:

- Were there any close calls or concerning moments?
- Did everyone follow the safety rules?
- What did you learn about the route?
- How can you be better prepared next time?

This debriefing helps children process the experience and reinforces safety lessons.

### Maintain Your Equipment

After each ride:

- Clean bikes to prevent rust and wear
- Check for damage or loose parts
- Store bikes properly away from weather
- Recharge lights and phones

Regular maintenance prevents mechanical failures on future rides.
