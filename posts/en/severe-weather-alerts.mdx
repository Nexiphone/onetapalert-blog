## Understanding Severe Weather Alerts and Warning Systems

The National Weather Service (NWS) issues various types of alerts to warn the public about dangerous weather conditions. Understanding the difference between these alerts is crucial for appropriate response.

### Types of Severe Weather Alerts

**Watches** indicate that conditions are favorable for severe weather to develop. When a watch is issued, you should stay informed and be prepared to take action quickly.

**Warnings** mean that severe weather has been detected or is imminent. This is your signal to take immediate protective action. Warnings are issued for tornadoes, severe thunderstorms, flash floods, and other dangerous conditions.

**Advisories** are issued for less serious conditions that may still cause inconvenience or pose minor threats to safety.

Understanding these distinctions helps you gauge the urgency of the situation. ### How Severe Weather Alerts Are Delivered

Today's technology provides multiple channels for receiving weather alerts:

- **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** sent directly to your phone
- **NOAA Weather Radio** broadcasts
- **TV and radio announcements**
- **Weather apps and services**
- **Outdoor warning sirens** in many communities

## What to Do When You Receive a Severe Weather Alert

Knowing how to respond appropriately to different types of severe weather alerts can save your life and the lives of those around you.

### Responding to Tornado Warnings

Tornadoes are among the most violent and unpredictable weather phenomena. When a tornado warning is issued for your area:

1. **Go to your designated shelter immediately**—typically a basement or an interior room on the lowest floor away from windows
2. **Get as low as possible** and cover your head and neck with your arms
3. **Alert your emergency contacts** using One Tap Alert's instant SOS button so they know you're taking shelter
4. **Stay in shelter** until the warning has expired

If you're caught outside or in a vehicle when a tornado warning is issued, never try to outrun a tornado. Seek shelter in a sturdy building immediately, and use One Tap Alert to notify your contacts of your situation and location.

### Responding to Flash Flood Warnings

Flash floods develop quickly and can be deadly. Water levels can rise several feet in minutes, and just six inches of fast-moving water can knock you off your feet.

When a flash flood warning is issued:

1. **Move to higher ground immediately**
2. **Never attempt to walk or drive through flooded areas**—turn around, don't drown
3. **Activate One Tap Alert** to share your location with emergency contacts
4. **If trapped in a building**, move to the highest floor (not the attic, where you could become trapped)
5. **If trapped in a vehicle**, abandon it and move to higher ground only if you can do so safely

The live GPS tracking feature in One Tap Alert is particularly crucial during flood events, as you may need to move rapidly through unfamiliar areas to reach safety. Your emergency contacts can follow your movement and coordinate rescue efforts if needed.

### Responding to Severe Thunderstorm Warnings

While thunderstorms may seem less threatening than tornadoes or floods, they can produce lightning, damaging winds, and large hail that pose serious risks.

When a severe thunderstorm warning is issued:

1. **Get indoors immediately** and stay away from windows
2. **Avoid using corded phones** or electrical appliances
3. **Stay informed** about the storm's progression
4. **Set a Safety Timer** on One Tap Alert if you must be outside before the storm passes

If you're caught outside with no shelter available, crouch low to the ground (but don't lie flat), avoid isolated trees or tall objects, and activate One Tap Alert so your contacts know your situation.

### Responding to Hurricane Warnings

Hurricanes provide more advance warning than other severe weather events, but they require extensive preparation and potentially evacuation.

When a hurricane warning is issued:

1. **Follow evacuation orders immediately** if issued for your area
2. **If evacuating**, set a Safety Timer on One Tap Alert for your estimated travel time
3. **Share your evacuation route and destination** with emergency contacts
4. **If sheltering in place**, ensure your emergency supplies are ready and One Tap Alert is easily accessible

During the hurricane, maintain communication with your emergency contact network through One Tap Alert. If conditions deteriorate rapidly or you encounter danger, the instant SOS button ensures immediate help notification.

Your emergency kit should be ready before severe weather threatens. Store it in your designated shelter area and ensure all family members know its location.

## After the Storm: Post-Severe Weather Safety

The danger doesn't always end when the weather clears. Post-storm hazards can be just as deadly as the event itself.

### Stay Safe After Severe Weather Passes

- **Wait for official all-clear** before leaving shelter
- **Watch for downed power lines** and report them immediately
- **Avoid flood water**, which may be contaminated or electrically charged
- **Document damage** with photos for insurance purposes
- **Check on neighbors**, especially elderly or vulnerable individuals
- **Use One Tap Alert's Safety Timer** if you need to venture into potentially hazardous areas to assess damage
