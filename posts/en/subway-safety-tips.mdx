## On the Platform

### Position Yourself Strategically

Stand in well-lit areas near other commuters or station attendants. Avoid standing too close to the platform edge, particularly when trains aren't arriving. Position yourself near the middle of the platform where station attendants or security cameras are more likely to be present.

Keep your phone accessible but secure. Having **One Tap Alert** on your home screen means you can activate the SOS button with just a press and hold for one second if needed, without fumbling through apps in an emergency.

### Stay Alert and Aware

Remove headphones or keep volume low enough to hear announcements and your surroundings. Avoid being absorbed in your phone screen. If you need to use your device, position yourself with your back against a wall or column so you can see approaching people.

This heightened awareness doesn't mean being paranoid—it means being present. And knowing you have **One Tap Alert** ready to connect you with your emergency contacts instantly can help you stay calm and confident rather than anxious.

### Trust Your Instincts

If someone makes you uncomfortable, move to a different area of the platform. If a particular train car looks unsafe or problematic, wait for the next train or choose a different car. Your intuition is a powerful safety tool—honor it.

## While Riding the Train

### Choose Your Car Wisely

When possible, board cars with other passengers, ideally those with diverse groups of commuters. The conductor's car (usually in the middle of the train) often has more activity and oversight. Avoid empty cars, especially during off-peak hours.

### Select a Strategic Seat

Sit near the conductor's cabin or close to exits if possible. Avoid sitting in isolated corners or against walls where you can't see the rest of the car. Aisle seats or seats facing the car's interior give you better visibility and easier exits.

### Keep Belongings Secure

Hold bags on your lap or between your feet, not on the seat beside you or on an overhead rack where they're vulnerable to quick thefts. Keep valuable items like phones, wallets, and jewelry concealed. If you need to check your phone, hold it securely with both hands.

### Maintain Situational Awareness

Continuously scan your environment. Notice who boards at each stop and any changes in the car's atmosphere. Be particularly alert during stops when doors are open and new passengers enter.

## Late-Night Subway Safety

Evening and late-night subway travel requires extra vigilance. Trains and platforms are less crowded, which means fewer witnesses and potentially longer waits for help.

### Wait in Designated Safe Areas

Many stations have designated off-hours waiting areas marked with signs or located near station attendant booths. Use these areas when traveling late at night.

### Set Up Protection Before You Travel

Before boarding a late-night train, activate **One Tap Alert's safety timer** and let trusted contacts know your expected arrival time. Consider using the **real-time location sharing** feature for your entire journey so someone is virtually "with you" throughout your commute.

### Stay Near Others

Even if you typically prefer quiet cars, late-night travel is the time to stay near groups of other commuters. There's safety in numbers, and conductors are more likely to pass through populated cars.

## Dealing With Harassment or Uncomfortable Situations

### Create Distance Immediately

If someone is making you uncomfortable, move away immediately. Change seats, switch cars at the next stop, or exit the train entirely if necessary. Your safety is more important than reaching your destination a few minutes sooner.

### Make Noise and Seek Help

Don't be afraid to speak firmly, make noise, or call for help if someone is harassing you. Alert the conductor, station attendant, or other passengers. Most commuters will support someone clearly in distress.

### Document and Report

If possible and safe to do so, take note of physical descriptions, badge numbers, or other identifying information. Report incidents to transit authorities. This helps protect not just you, but other passengers as well.

## When to Avoid Subway Travel

Sometimes the safest decision is to choose alternative transportation:

- When stations or lines are known to have ongoing safety issues
- During service disruptions that would leave you in unfamiliar areas
- When you feel unwell or impaired and might not be alert
- During extreme weather that affects underground safety (flooding risks)
- When your instinct strongly tells you something isn't right

Having a backup plan—whether that's a rideshare app, a friend's number, or emergency cash for a taxi—ensures you're never forced into an unsafe situation.
