## Understanding Home Invasions: What Makes Them Different

Home invasions differ fundamentally from standard burglaries. While burglars typically target empty homes to steal property, home invaders know occupants are present and may have more sinister intentions including assault, kidnapping, or creating hostage situations.

Key characteristics of home invasions include:

- **Forced entry** while residents are home (usually evening or early morning)
- **Multiple perpetrators** working together
- **Use of violence or threats** to control victims
- **Time-sensitive nature** requiring immediate response decisions

The FBI reports that most home invasions last between 90 seconds and 12 minutes, making rapid response absolutely critical. Having a plan before an emergency occurs dramatically increases your chances of staying safe.

## Immediate Response: The First 60 Seconds

Your actions in the first minute of a home invasion are crucial. Here's what to do immediately:

### Stay Calm and Assess Quickly

Panic is natural, but controlled thinking saves lives. Within the first few seconds:

- **Listen carefully** to determine how many intruders and their location
- **Don't confront** unless absolutely necessary for escape
- **Protect family members** by using pre-established code words or signals

### Alert Authorities Immediately

The faster you can contact emergency services and trusted contacts, the better your outcome. This is where having **One Tap Alert** pre-configured on your phone becomes invaluable. Instead of fumbling to dial 911 or text family members, a single press and hold for 1 second instantly alerts all your emergency contacts with your exact location via live GPS tracking.

### Find Safe Position or Exit

If intruders haven't seen you yet:

- **Move to a pre-designated safe room** (ideally with a lock and cell phone)
- **Exit the home immediately** if you have a clear escape route
- **Hide only as a last resort** if escape and securing yourself in a safe room aren't possible

The key is having these decisions mapped out beforehand, so you're not making life-or-death choices under extreme stress.

## Creating Your Home Invasion Response Plan

Every household needs a specific, practiced emergency response plan. Here's how to build yours:

### Designate Safe Rooms

Identify one room in your home that can serve as a safe room:

- **Ideally bathroom or bedroom** with locking door
- **Keep a charged phone** in this room at all times
- **Include emergency supplies**: phone charger, flashlight, water
- **Have an alternative exit** if possible (window with escape ladder on second floor)

### Establish Emergency Contact Protocols

Your emergency contact list should include:

- **Local police non-emergency number** (saved in phone)
- **Trusted neighbors** who can respond quickly
- **Family members** who should be notified
- **Friends or colleagues** who can serve as emergency contacts

With **One Tap Alert**, you can add unlimited emergency contacts who will all receive simultaneous alerts with your real-time location sharing when you activate the SOS button. This ensures multiple people know you're in danger and can coordinate response efforts while you focus on staying safe.

### Teach All Household Members

Everyone in your home should know:

- **Location of safe rooms** and primary/secondary escape routes
- **Code words** that indicate danger without alerting intruders
- **How to activate emergency alerts** using apps like One Tap Alert
- **Where to meet** outside if separated during evacuation

Practice your plan at least twice a year. Just like fire drills, home invasion drills help everyone respond automatically during high-stress situations.

## During the Invasion: Critical Do's and Don'ts

### DO:

- **Cooperate if confronted** - property is replaceable, your life isn't
- **Observe details** - height, clothing, voice, distinguishing features
- **Keep hands visible** - avoid movements that could be perceived as threats
- **Speak calmly** - if forced to communicate with intruders
- **Use your SOS button** - activate One Tap Alert the moment you can do so safely and discreetly

### DON'T:

- **Don't be a hero** - attempting to fight multiple armed intruders is extremely dangerous
- **Don't make sudden movements** - these can trigger violent responses
- **Don't look directly at faces** if possible - some intruders react violently if they feel identified
- **Don't argue or negotiate** - comply with reasonable demands for your safety
- **Don't assume it's over** - remain cautious even after apparent departure

## After the Invasion: Critical Next Steps

Once intruders have left and you're safe:

### Immediate Actions

1. **Contact police if not already notified** - even if someone else called, make a direct report
2. **Don't touch anything** - preserve the crime scene for evidence collection
3. **Document everything** - write down all details while memory is fresh
4. **Seek medical attention** - even if injuries seem minor, get evaluated
5. **Notify your bank and credit card companies** - if wallets, purses, or financial documents were taken

### Follow-Up Within 24-48 Hours

- **File insurance claims** with documented evidence
- **Change all locks and security codes** immediately
- **Install or upgrade security systems** if needed
- **Review your emergency response** and identify areas for improvement
- **Consider counseling services** - trauma response is normal and professional support helps

Your **One Tap Alert** account will have stored your emergency event data, including exact times and locations, which can assist with police reports and insurance documentation.

## Prevention: Hardening Your Home Against Invasion

While no home is completely invasion-proof, these measures significantly reduce your risk:

### Physical Security Measures

- **Solid core doors** with deadbolts on all exterior entrances
- **Reinforced door frames** with 3-inch strike plates and screws
- **Window locks** on all accessible windows
- **Motion-activated lighting** covering all entry points
- **Security cameras** with cloud storage (doorbell cams, exterior cameras)
- **Alarm system** with window/door sensors and loud alarm

### Behavioral Security Practices

- **Always lock doors** even when home
- **Don't advertise valuables** - keep curtains closed at night
- **Vary your routine** - don't establish predictable patterns
- **Know your neighbors** - strong community ties improve safety
- **Don't post vacation plans** on social media until you return
- **Keep One Tap Alert configured** with current emergency contacts and test periodically
