## Why Public Restroom Safety Matters

Public restrooms pose distinct safety concerns because they're semi-private spaces where you're temporarily isolated from others. These locations can attract individuals with bad intentions precisely because people are alone and distracted. Additionally, the layout of many public facilities—with limited visibility, multiple entry points, and soundproofing—can make it difficult for others to notice if something goes wrong.

According to safety experts, public restrooms rank among the top locations where people feel vulnerable in urban environments. Understanding this risk isn't about living in fear—it's about being prepared and taking reasonable precautions that can make all the difference.

## Assessing Safety Before Entering

Your safety strategy should begin before you even walk through the restroom door. Taking a moment to assess the situation can help you make informed decisions.

### Check the Location and Surroundings

Before entering any public restroom, observe your surroundings. Is the facility well-lit and well-maintained? Are other people around, or is the area isolated? Restrooms in busy, high-traffic areas tend to be safer than those in secluded locations.

If you're in an unfamiliar area, **One Tap Alert's Safety Timer feature** allows you to set a countdown before entering the restroom. If you don't check back in within your designated time frame, your emergency contacts are automatically alerted with your live location—giving you peace of mind that someone will notice if something is wrong.

### Trust Your Instincts

Your intuition is a powerful safety tool. If something feels off about a particular restroom—whether it's the location, the people loitering nearby, or just a gut feeling—trust that instinct and find an alternative. It's always better to walk a bit farther to find a safer option than to ignore warning signs.

### Choose Facilities Wisely

When possible, opt for restrooms in well-established businesses like coffee shops, hotels, or department stores rather than isolated park facilities or gas stations in remote areas. Establishments with employees present and security cameras are generally safer options.

## Safety Strategies While Inside Public Restrooms

Once you've decided to use a public restroom, several practical strategies can enhance your safety while inside.

### Keep Your Phone Accessible

Never put your phone deep in your bag or pocket where you can't reach it quickly. Keep it in your hand or in an easily accessible pocket. With **One Tap Alert**, you can activate the instant SOS button by pressing and holding for just one second—immediately alerting all your emergency contacts with your live GPS location.

This feature is specifically designed for situations where you need help fast but can't make a lengthy phone call. In a public restroom emergency, those seconds matter.

### Lock the Stall Door Immediately

This might seem obvious, but always lock the stall door as soon as you enter. Check that the lock is functioning properly before getting situated. A properly locked door is your first line of defense and gives you time to respond if someone tries to enter.

### Stay Alert and Aware

Avoid becoming completely absorbed in your phone or other distractions while using public facilities. Keep your ears open for unusual sounds, multiple people entering, or someone lingering outside your stall. Staying situationally aware allows you to notice potential problems before they escalate.

### Position Yourself Strategically

When possible, choose a middle stall rather than the first or last one, as these tend to be used less frequently and may feel more isolated. However, if you feel uncomfortable with people on both sides, the end stall might provide a better escape route.

### Keep Belongings Secure

Never hang your bag, purse, or jacket on the door hook where it can be grabbed from above or below the stall. Keep your belongings on your lap or use a floor hook if available. Thieves sometimes target public restrooms specifically because people are distracted and vulnerable.

## What to Do If You Feel Unsafe

Despite taking precautions, you may occasionally find yourself feeling unsafe in a public restroom. Here's what to do:

### Exit Immediately If Possible

If you feel threatened and haven't yet committed to using the facility, simply leave. You don't owe anyone an explanation. Your safety is more important than avoiding awkwardness.

### Activate Your SOS Alert

If you're in immediate danger, press and hold the **One Tap Alert SOS button** for one second. This instantly notifies your emergency contacts with your live location. Your contacts can then call 911, call the establishment you're in, or come to help you directly.

### Make Noise

If someone is attempting to enter your stall or harassing you, make noise. Yell "NO," "STOP," or "HELP" loudly. This often startles attackers and alerts others nearby that something is wrong.

### Call for Help

If you have time and feel safe doing so, call 911 directly. However, if making a phone call might escalate the situation, using One Tap Alert's silent SOS feature may be the safer option.

### Stay in the Stall

If someone threatening is outside your stall, stay locked inside and call for help. Don't open the door until police or security arrives, even if the person claims to be leaving or tries to convince you everything is fine.
