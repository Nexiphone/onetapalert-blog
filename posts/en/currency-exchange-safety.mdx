# Currency Exchange Safety: 10 Essential Tips to Protect Yourself While Traveling

Exchanging currency is an unavoidable part of international travel, but it's also a vulnerable moment that can expose you to theft, scams, and personal safety risks. Whether you're withdrawing cash from an ATM in a foreign city or visiting a currency exchange booth, knowing how to protect yourself is crucial for a safe and stress-free trip.

## Why Currency Exchange Situations Are High-Risk

Currency exchange creates a perfect storm of vulnerability factors. You're often in an unfamiliar location, distracted while counting money, carrying larger-than-usual amounts of cash, and potentially visible to opportunistic criminals who target tourists at exchange locations.

According to travel security experts, tourists are 3-4 times more likely to be targeted for theft immediately after visiting currency exchange locations or ATMs. Criminals know you're carrying cash, you may be disoriented in a new environment, and you're probably focused on your transaction rather than your surroundings.

## 10 Essential Currency Exchange Safety Tips

### 1. Exchange During Daylight Hours

Whenever possible, handle your currency exchange during busy daylight hours when there are more people around and better visibility. Avoid late-night ATM visits or currency exchanges in isolated areas.

If you must exchange currency during less optimal hours, activate **One Tap Alert's live location sharing** feature so your emergency contacts can see exactly where you are in real-time. This GPS tracking provides an extra security layer, knowing someone is aware of your whereabouts.

### 2. Use ATMs in Secure, Well-Lit Locations

The safest ATMs are those inside bank branches, shopping malls, hotels, or busy commercial areas. Avoid standalone ATMs in isolated or poorly lit locations, especially at night.

Before using any ATM, take a moment to assess your surroundings. If something feels off, trust your instincts and find a different location. Having **One Tap Alert** ready on your phone means you can press and hold the SOS button for just one second if you sense danger, immediately alerting all your emergency contacts with your exact location.

### 3. Be Aware of Your Surroundings

Stay alert before, during, and after your transaction. Look for anyone watching you or lingering nearby without apparent purpose. Criminals often work in teams, with one person monitoring the exchange location while another follows targets.

This is where One Tap Alert's **instant SOS button** becomes invaluable. If you notice suspicious behavior or feel threatened, you don't need to unlock your phone, navigate through apps, or type a message. Just press and hold the SOS button, and your emergency network is instantly notified.

### 4. Count Your Money Discreetly and Verify Immediately

Always count your money before leaving the exchange counter or ATM, but do so discreetly. Openly displaying large amounts of cash advertises you as a target. If there's a discrepancy, address it immediately while still at the location.

Once you've verified your cash, quickly store it securely in a money belt, hidden pocket, or divided between multiple secure locations on your person. Don't count it again in public view.

### 5. Never Accept Help from Strangers at ATMs

A common scam involves someone offering to "help" you with an ATM, then stealing your card, PIN, or cash. Politely decline any assistance and if someone persists, cancel your transaction and leave.

### 7. Use the Buddy System When Possible

Travel with a companion when exchanging currency, especially large amounts. One person can complete the transaction while the other watches for suspicious activity and monitors the surroundings.

Rather than exchanging large sums all at once, make smaller exchanges as needed throughout your trip. This reduces both your visibility as a target and the potential loss if something does go wrong.

If a location, situation, or person makes you uncomfortable, listen to that inner warning. Leave and find an alternative. Your safety is always more important than convenience or a slightly better exchange rate.
