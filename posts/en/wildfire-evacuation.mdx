## Understanding Wildfire Evacuation Zones and Alerts

Before wildfire season arrives, familiarize yourself with your local evacuation zone system. Most fire-prone regions use a tiered alert system:

- **Evacuation Warning (Set/Ready)**: Conditions are deteriorating. Prepare to leave immediately.
- **Evacuation Order (Go)**: Immediate threat. Leave NOW.
- **Shelter in Place**: Only issued when leaving is more dangerous than staying.

Sign up for your county's emergency alert system, monitor local news, and download weather apps that provide fire tracking information. However, official alerts don't always reach everyone instantly, which is where personal safety apps become crucial.

## Create Your Wildfire Evacuation Plan Before Disaster Strikes

### Identify Multiple Evacuation Routes

Never rely on a single escape route. Wildfires are unpredictable and can block roads within minutes. Map out at least three different routes from your home to safety, including:

- Primary highway routes
- Back roads and alternative paths
- Routes that avoid canyon roads (fire traps) and dead ends

Drive these routes during non-emergency times so they become second nature. Store physical maps in your vehicle—GPS and cell service often fail during wildfires.

### Designate Safe Meeting Points

Establish multiple meeting locations where family members can reunite if separated:

- A nearby location (park, school parking lot)
- An out-of-neighborhood location
- An out-of-town contact person

This is where **One Tap Alert** becomes invaluable. Rather than wondering if everyone made it to the meeting point safely, family members added as emergency contacts can see each other's live GPS locations in real-time, eliminating dangerous guesswork during chaotic evacuations.

## Assemble Your Wildfire Evacuation Kit

Your go-bag should be ready to grab within seconds. Store it in an easily accessible location near your exit door. Essential items include:

### Immediate Necessities
- Water (1 gallon per person per day for 3 days)
- Non-perishable food for 3 days
- Medications and prescriptions
- First aid kit
- Battery-powered or hand-crank radio
- Flashlights and extra batteries
- Phone chargers (including car chargers and portable battery banks)

### Important Items
- Cash and credit cards
- Change of clothes and sturdy shoes
- Copies of important documents (or have them in your One Tap Alert Secure Vault)
- N95 or P100 respirator masks
- Goggles to protect eyes from smoke and ash
- Pet carriers and supplies

### Comfort Items
- Blankets and sleeping bags
- Personal hygiene items
- Items for infants, elderly, or disabled family members
- Comfort items for children

Keep your vehicle's gas tank at least half full during fire season—gas stations may lose power or have long lines during evacuations.

## Recognizing When to Evacuate

### Don't Wait for Official Orders

While official evacuation orders should always be obeyed immediately, sometimes you need to make the call yourself. Evacuate if you observe:

- Visible flames or smoke approaching your area
- Ash falling on your property
- Strong winds combined with extreme heat
- Difficulty breathing due to smoke
- Authorities going door-to-door
- Animals fleeing the area

Trust your instincts. It's better to evacuate early and unnecessarily than to wait too long and face deadly consequences.

### The "Ready, Set, Go" Principle

- **Ready**: Always be prepared with your evacuation kit, plan, and emergency apps like **One Tap Alert** installed and configured with your emergency contacts
- **Set**: When a warning is issued, prepare to leave at a moment's notice
- **Go**: Leave immediately when ordered—do not hesitate

## During the Evacuation: Critical Safety Steps

### Before Leaving Your Home

If time permits (but never delay if fire is imminent):

- Close all windows and doors but leave them unlocked
- Remove flammable window coverings and furniture from windows
- Move flammable furniture to the center of rooms
- Shut off gas at the meter
- Leave exterior lights on
- Wear protective clothing (long pants, long-sleeved shirt, boots)

### While Driving Through Fire Areas

- Keep headlights on
- Drive slowly with windows closed
- Watch for other evacuees and emergency vehicles
- If you encounter heavy smoke, use low-beam headlights
- If you become trapped in your vehicle, stay inside, cover yourself with a blanket, lie on the floor, and call 911

Activate **One Tap Alert** before you leave so contacts can monitor your journey. If you need to update your status or let people know you've encountered obstacles, the app maintains that critical communication link even when phone lines are jammed.

## After You've Evacuated: Staying Safe and Connected

Only return home when authorities declare it safe. Hidden dangers like hot spots, weakened structures, and contaminated water systems may not be immediately visible.
