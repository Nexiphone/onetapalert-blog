Staying in an Airbnb can be an incredible travel experience—offering unique accommodations, local flavor, and often better value than traditional hotels. But with the freedom and privacy of a rental property comes responsibility for your own safety. Unlike hotels with 24/7 front desks and security staff, Airbnb guests are essentially on their own in unfamiliar locations.

Whether you're traveling solo, with friends, or as a family, understanding Airbnb safety risks and taking proactive precautions can make the difference between a memorable trip and a dangerous situation. ## Before You Book: Vetting Your Airbnb

### Research the Property Thoroughly

The first step in Airbnb safety happens before you ever click "book." Take time to thoroughly research potential properties. Read all reviews carefully, paying special attention to comments about safety, neighborhood quality, and host responsiveness. Look for patterns in reviews—multiple guests mentioning the same concern is a red flag.

Check the property's exact location on a map. Is it in a well-lit area? Are there businesses or residential buildings nearby? Use Google Street View to get a visual sense of the neighborhood during daytime and, if available, nighttime.

### Verify Host Credentials

Legitimate hosts typically have verified profiles, positive reviews spanning multiple years, and prompt communication. Be wary of hosts with brand-new accounts, no reviews, or who are reluctant to answer safety-related questions. A trustworthy host should be happy to provide additional information about locks, security systems, and emergency procedures.

### Check for Safety Amenities

Review the listing for essential safety features: smoke detectors, carbon monoxide detectors, fire extinguishers, first aid kits, and secure locks on all doors and windows. Properties without these basic safety amenities should be avoided. Don't hesitate to message the host directly to confirm these items are present and functional.

## Upon Arrival: Securing Your Space

### Inspect the Property Immediately

When you first arrive at your Airbnb, conduct a thorough safety inspection before settling in. Test all door and window locks to ensure they work properly. Check that smoke and carbon monoxide detectors are present and have fresh batteries. Locate all exits and plan your escape route in case of emergency.

Look for any hidden cameras in private spaces like bedrooms and bathrooms—while rare, this has been reported. Check unusual objects like smoke detectors, clocks, or decorative items that face beds or showers.

### Know Your Emergency Exits

Familiarize yourself with all exits from the property. In an apartment building, locate the fire stairs. In a house, identify which windows could serve as emergency exits. Keep a flashlight by your bed in case of power outages.

### Document Everything

Take photos and videos of the property when you arrive, including all locks, safety equipment, and the general condition of the space. This protects you if there are disputes later and creates a record of the property's initial state. Share these with a trusted friend or family member back home.

## During Your Stay: Ongoing Safety Practices

### Maintain Privacy About Your Location

While it's tempting to share every moment on social media, avoid posting real-time updates about your Airbnb location or indicating that you're traveling alone. Save the vacation photos for after you've returned home. Don't discuss your accommodation details with strangers or in public spaces where others might overhear.

Instead of broadcasting to social media, use One Tap Alert to share your real-time location with a select group of trusted emergency contacts. This gives the people who matter most—your family and close friends—the ability to check on your whereabouts without alerting potential bad actors to your travel plans.

### Keep Your Host at Arm's Length

While most Airbnb hosts are respectful and professional, maintain appropriate boundaries. You're not obligated to share personal information, your travel itinerary, or let the host enter the property without notice (except in genuine emergencies). If a host makes you uncomfortable with excessive contact or unannounced visits, document the interactions and report them to Airbnb.

### Lock Up Every Time

This seems obvious, but it's easy to become complacent during a relaxing vacation. Lock all doors and windows every time you leave the property and when you're inside—especially at night. If the provided locks seem insufficient, consider bringing a portable door lock or door wedge for added security.

### Trust Your Instincts

If something feels off about the property, neighborhood, or host, trust that feeling. You're under no obligation to stay somewhere you don't feel safe. Contact Airbnb support immediately if you have legitimate safety concerns—they can help you relocate to a different property.

## Special Considerations for Solo Travelers

### Share Your Itinerary with Trusted Contacts

Solo travelers should always ensure someone back home knows their exact location and schedule. Share your Airbnb address, check-in and check-out dates, and general daily plans with a trusted friend or family member. Establish regular check-in times so they'll know if something is wrong.

### Choose Strategically Located Properties

Solo travelers should prioritize properties in busy, well-populated areas over isolated locations, even if they cost more. Properties near public transportation, restaurants, and shops provide both convenience and safety. Avoid ground-floor apartments with easily accessible windows.

### Establish Safety Routines

Create consistent safety habits during your stay. Check in with loved ones at the same time daily. ## Safety Tips for International Airbnb Stays

### Research Local Emergency Numbers

911 doesn't work everywhere. Before traveling internationally, research local emergency service numbers and save them in your phone. Many countries use 112 as their universal emergency number, but verify this for your specific destination.

Program these numbers into One Tap Alert alongside your emergency contacts, and ensure your contacts know what country you're in so they can alert local authorities if needed.

### Understand Cultural Safety Norms

Safety practices and risks vary significantly by country and culture. Research local safety concerns specific to your destination. Some areas have higher rates of certain crimes, different traffic safety standards, or cultural practices around gender and solo travel that affect your safety strategy.

### Arrange Safe Airport Transfers

Some of the most vulnerable moments in international Airbnb travel occur during arrival and departure transfers. Pre-arrange transportation through reputable services rather than accepting rides from strangers at the airport. Share your transfer details with emergency contacts and use One Tap Alert's live location sharing during the journey.

### Keep Embassy Information Handy

Store your country's embassy or consulate contact information for your destination. In serious emergencies involving crime, legal issues, or lost documents, your embassy should be one of your first calls.

## Red Flags: When to Cancel or Leave an Airbnb

Some situations warrant immediately leaving an Airbnb, even if it means losing your booking fee:

- **Unsafe neighborhood**: If you arrive and the area feels genuinely dangerous, don't stay out of financial obligation
- **Security failures**: Missing or broken locks, non-functional safety equipment, or easy unauthorized access to the property
- **Host boundary violations**: Unannounced entries, hidden cameras, or harassment
- **Misrepresented property**: Major discrepancies between listing photos and reality, especially regarding safety features
- **Gut feeling**: If you genuinely fear for your safety, that's reason enough to leave

Document everything, contact Airbnb support immediately, and relocate to a hotel if necessary. Your safety is worth more than any accommodation cost.
