## Understanding the Risks at Busy Intersections

Busy intersections present multiple hazards simultaneously. Vehicles approach from multiple directions, pedestrians cross at various angles, cyclists weave through traffic, and public transit adds another layer of complexity. According to traffic safety research, intersection-related crashes account for approximately 40% of all urban accidents.

The most common dangers include:

- **Distracted drivers** checking phones or navigation systems
- **Red light runners** who speed through intersections
- **Right-turn-on-red vehicles** that fail to yield to pedestrians
- **Limited visibility** due to large vehicles, construction, or weather conditions
- **Confusing traffic patterns** at multi-lane intersections
- **Aggressive pedestrians** who jaywalk or ignore signals

Understanding these risks is the first step toward protecting yourself. ## Essential Tips for Navigating Busy Intersections Safely

### Make Eye Contact with Drivers

Never assume a driver sees you, even if you have the right of way. Before stepping into a crosswalk, make direct eye contact with drivers who might turn across your path. This simple action confirms that the driver has acknowledged your presence.

If you cannot make eye contact—perhaps because of tinted windows or sun glare—assume the driver hasn't seen you and wait for them to pass. When crossing, keep your head up and maintain awareness of all approaching vehicles. Having **One Tap Alert** ready on your phone means that if a driver does behave erratically or you sense danger, you can quickly activate the SOS button to alert your emergency contacts of your exact location.

### Follow Traffic Signals Completely

It might seem obvious, but many pedestrian injuries occur because people don't follow signals properly. Don't start crossing when the countdown timer is already running low. Wait for the full walk signal before entering the intersection.

Additionally, don't assume traffic will stop immediately when your light turns green. Give vehicles a few seconds to clear the intersection, as some drivers accelerate through yellow lights or run red lights entirely. This "defensive crossing" approach adds several seconds to your journey but significantly reduces your risk.

### Stay Visible and Alert

Visibility is crucial, especially during early morning hours, evenings, or in bad weather. Wear bright or reflective clothing when possible. If you're a regular commuter who crosses busy intersections during dark hours, consider carrying a small flashlight or wearing reflective accessories.

Equally important is staying alert yourself. Remove headphones or keep volume low enough to hear approaching vehicles. Never text or look at your phone while crossing. Your full attention should be on the traffic environment around you. Save phone interactions for when you've reached safety—except, of course, if you need to activate **One Tap Alert's** instant SOS button in an emergency situation.

### Use Designated Crosswalks

Jaywalking or crossing mid-block at busy intersections dramatically increases your risk. Drivers don't expect pedestrians in these locations, reducing reaction time if you step into traffic. Always use marked crosswalks and pedestrian signals.

At particularly complex intersections, look for pedestrian bridges or underpasses. While they may add time to your route, they eliminate the risk of vehicle interaction entirely.

### Be Extra Cautious at Right Turns

Vehicles turning right on red create a specific hazard for pedestrians. Drivers often focus on oncoming traffic from their left while neglecting to check the crosswalk to their right. If you're crossing in front of a vehicle waiting to turn right, assume they haven't seen you.

Wait for clear acknowledgment from the driver or for them to complete their turn before you enter the crosswalk. When you do cross, stay to the far edge of the crosswalk away from turning vehicles.

### Watch for Multiple Threats

At busy intersections, danger can come from multiple directions simultaneously. A stopped car might block your view of an approaching cyclist. A bus pulling away might obscure a vehicle running a red light. Always scan the entire intersection—left, right, and ahead—throughout your crossing.

This is especially important at intersections with multiple turn lanes or slip lanes that allow continuous right turns. Never become complacent, even at familiar intersections you cross daily.

### Plan Your Route Strategically

When possible, plan routes that avoid the busiest, most dangerous intersections during peak traffic hours. Sometimes a slightly longer route with safer crossings is worth the extra time. Use mapping apps to identify intersections with pedestrian signals, longer crossing times, or better visibility.

For regular commuters, varying your route occasionally can also reduce predictability, which is a separate safety consideration for personal security. ## Additional Intersection Safety Considerations

### Weather-Related Precautions

Bad weather amplifies intersection dangers. Rain, snow, fog, and ice all reduce visibility and increase stopping distances for vehicles. During adverse weather, allow extra time for crossing, make yourself even more visible, and assume drivers have reduced ability to stop quickly.

Puddles at intersections can also obscure road markings and make it difficult to judge curb heights. Step carefully, and remember that wet leaves in autumn can be as slippery as ice.

### Night-Time Intersection Crossing

Intersections are significantly more dangerous after dark. Reduced visibility affects both pedestrians and drivers. If you regularly cross busy intersections at night, consider carrying a small flashlight or wearing reflective gear. Some commuters keep a reflective vest in their bag for evening walks.

Before crossing, step into well-lit areas where drivers can clearly see you. Avoid crossing from between parked cars where you might be invisible until you're already in the roadway. Having **One Tap Alert** readily accessible on your phone is especially important during night-time commutes when help might be less immediately available.

### Teaching Intersection Safety to Children and Teens

If you have children or teens who navigate urban intersections, teaching them proper crossing techniques is critical. Young people are often more distracted, less aware of traffic patterns, and more likely to take risks.

Consider setting up **One Tap Alert** on your teen's phone so they have immediate access to emergency help if needed. ### Intersection Safety for Cyclists and Scooter Riders

If you navigate busy intersections on a bicycle or electric scooter, you face unique challenges. You're faster than pedestrians but more vulnerable than cars, occupying an in-between space that drivers don't always anticipate.

Follow all traffic signals as if you were a vehicle. Make yourself visible with lights and reflective gear. Never assume drivers see you, especially in their blind spots. Position yourself clearly in lanes rather than riding the edge where drivers might try to squeeze past.
