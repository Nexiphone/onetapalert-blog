## What Is a Go-Bag and Why Do You Need One?

A go-bag (also called a bug-out bag, 72-hour kit, or emergency evacuation kit) is a portable collection of essential items you need to survive independently for at least 72 hours during an emergency. The goal is simple: grab it and go, knowing you have everything critical for your immediate survival.

Recent years have shown us that emergencies happen more frequently than we'd like to think—wildfires, hurricanes, floods, earthquakes, power grid failures, and even public safety emergencies. When evacuation orders come, you may have just minutes to leave. That's why your go-bag should be ready now, not when disaster strikes.

Beyond physical supplies, modern emergency preparedness also means having digital tools ready. ## Essential Go-Bag Categories: What to Pack

### Water and Hydration Supplies

Water is your most critical resource. Without it, you can only survive three days in moderate conditions.

**What to pack:**
- At least 1 gallon of water per person per day (3 gallons total for a 72-hour kit)
- Water purification tablets or a portable water filter
- Collapsible water bottles or hydration bladders
- Electrolyte packets for rehydration

**Pro tip:** Rotate your water supply every six months to ensure freshness. Before you head out on any evacuation or outdoor emergency scenario, use the **One Tap Alert Safety Timer** to set automatic check-ins—if you don't respond, your emergency contacts will be notified with your live GPS location and can coordinate rescue or support.

### Non-Perishable Food

You need calorie-dense, long-lasting food that requires minimal preparation.

**What to pack:**
- Ready-to-eat meals (MREs or freeze-dried camping meals)
- Energy bars and protein bars
- Dried fruits and nuts
- Peanut butter or almond butter packets
- Jerky or canned meats
- Comfort food (chocolate, hard candy) for morale

Choose foods with a shelf life of at least 1-2 years, and remember to accommodate dietary restrictions or allergies. Mark your calendar to check expiration dates annually.

### First Aid and Medical Supplies

Medical emergencies don't wait for convenient times, and professional help may not be immediately available.

**What to pack:**
- Comprehensive first aid kit with bandages, gauze, medical tape
- Antiseptic wipes and antibiotic ointment
- Pain relievers (ibuprofen, acetaminophen)
- Prescription medications (7-day supply minimum)
- Allergy medications and EpiPens if needed
- Tourniquet and trauma supplies
- Tweezers, scissors, safety pins
- Thermometer
- Personal hygiene items

Store copies of prescriptions and medical information in both physical and digital formats. The **One Tap Alert Secure Vault** feature is perfect for this—use its end-to-end encrypted storage to keep digital copies of insurance cards, prescriptions, medical history, and emergency medical information accessible even if you lose your physical documents.

### Clothing and Protection

Weather can change rapidly during emergencies, and you may be exposed to elements for extended periods.

**What to pack:**
- Change of clothes appropriate for your climate
- Sturdy, broken-in shoes or boots
- Rain gear and waterproof poncho
- Warm layers (fleece, thermal underwear)
- Hat, gloves, and warm socks
- Emergency blanket or compact sleeping bag
- Work gloves for handling debris

Choose moisture-wicking, quick-dry fabrics when possible. Pack for the season, but include some warm layers regardless—nights can get cold even in summer.

## Critical Tools and Equipment

### Light and Communication

When the power goes out or you're evacuating at night, proper lighting is essential.

**What to pack:**
- LED headlamp with extra batteries
- Flashlight with backup batteries
- Glow sticks (12-hour duration)
- Hand-crank emergency radio (with NOAA weather alerts)
- Portable phone charger or solar charger
- Whistle for signaling

### Multi-Purpose Tools

Versatile tools help you solve unexpected problems without carrying excessive weight.

**What to pack:**
- Swiss Army knife or multi-tool
- Duct tape (wrap around a pencil to save space)
- Paracord (50-100 feet)
- Fire starter (waterproof matches, lighter, ferro rod)
- Emergency shelter or tarp
- Zip ties and safety pins
- Small sewing kit

### Navigation and Signaling

GPS may fail, cell towers may be down, and you might need to navigate unfamiliar territory.

**What to pack:**
- Physical maps of your region (don't rely solely on phone maps)
- Compass
- Reflective emergency blanket (doubles as signaling device)
- Mirror for signaling
- Flares or road flares

## Important Documents and Cash

### Document Protection

You'll need proof of identity, ownership, and insurance when the emergency passes.

**What to pack (physical copies in waterproof container):**
- Photo ID and passport copies
- Insurance policies (home, auto, health)
- Bank account information
- Property deeds or rental agreements
- Family emergency contact list
- Photos of family members (for reunification purposes)
- Pet vaccination records

**What to store digitally:**
- All of the above, plus medical records
- Prescriptions and medication lists
- Recent photos of your home and valuables (for insurance claims)

### Emergency Cash

Digital payment systems may be unavailable during disasters when power grids fail.

**What to pack:**
- $200-500 in small bills ($1, $5, $10, $20)
- Quarters for pay phones or vending machines
- Additional cash if you have specific needs

## Personal and Comfort Items

Emergency situations are already stressful—small comforts can significantly impact mental resilience.

**What to pack:**
- Photographs of loved ones
- Small book or deck of cards
- Notebook and pencil
- Personal hygiene items (toothbrush, toothpaste, soap)
- Feminine hygiene products
- Glasses or contact lens supplies
- Baby supplies if applicable (diapers, formula, bottles)
- Pet supplies if applicable (food, leash, medications)

## Go-Bag Maintenance and Best Practices

Building your go-bag is just the first step. Proper maintenance ensures it's actually ready when you need it.

### Quarterly Review Schedule

Set recurring reminders to:
- Check expiration dates on food, water, and medications
- Test flashlights and replace batteries
- Update seasonal clothing
- Verify that all emergency contact information in **One Tap Alert** is current

### Practice Runs

Twice a year, conduct a practice evacuation:
1. Set a **One Tap Alert Safety Timer** for your practice duration
2. Grab only your go-bag and evacuate your home
3. Time how long it takes to reach your predetermined evacuation destination
4. Check what items you actually used or wished you had
5. Update your go-bag accordingly

This practice reveals gaps in your preparation and makes the real event less stressful.

### Location Strategy

Store your go-bag where you can grab it in seconds:
- Near your primary exit door
- In a climate-controlled area (not a hot garage or freezing shed)
- In a waterproof container or backpack
- Accessible to all family members who might need it

Make sure **One Tap Alert** is installed on every family member's iPhone with their emergency contacts configured. Test the SOS button together so everyone knows how it works before an actual emergency.

### Weight Considerations

Your go-bag should be portable—aim for 15-20% of your body weight maximum. If you need more supplies, create staged caches:
- **Immediate grab-and-go bag**: 72-hour essentials (15-20 lbs)
- **Vehicle bag**: Additional supplies stored in your car
- **Home base supplies**: Longer-term supplies if you shelter in place
