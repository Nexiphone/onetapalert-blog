## Understanding Winter Storm Dangers

Winter storms pose multiple threats that can quickly become life-threatening. Blizzards can reduce visibility to near zero, making travel extremely hazardous. Ice storms create treacherous walking and driving conditions, leading to accidents and falls. Freezing temperatures can cause hypothermia and frostbite in minutes, while power outages can leave homes without heat for extended periods.

According to the National Weather Service, winter storms cause an average of 1,300 deaths and 116,800 injuries annually in the United States. Many of these tragedies are preventable with proper preparation and the ability to quickly alert emergency contacts when help is needed. Having a reliable emergency communication system, such as the **One Tap Alert** SOS button on your smartphone, ensures you can call for help even when conditions deteriorate rapidly.

## Pre-Storm Preparation: Building Your Winter Emergency Kit

The time to prepare for a winter storm is well before the first snowflake falls. Creating a comprehensive emergency kit gives you peace of mind and the resources you need to weather the storm safely.

### Essential Supplies for Your Home

Stock your home with at least three days' worth of supplies for every family member. This includes non-perishable food, bottled water (one gallon per person per day), prescription medications, and first aid supplies. Don't forget battery-powered or hand-crank radios, flashlights with extra batteries, blankets, and winter clothing.

Keep important documents in a secure, accessible location. If you need to evacuate or emergency responders need access to your medical history, having these documents readily available on your phone can be invaluable.

### Winterizing Your Vehicle

Your vehicle emergency kit should include blankets, warm clothing, a shovel, ice scraper, jumper cables, flashlight, first aid kit, non-perishable snacks, and water. Add sand or cat litter for traction if you get stuck, and keep your gas tank at least half full during winter months.

Before heading out in winter weather, always tell someone your route and expected arrival time. ## During the Storm: Safety Protocols to Follow

When a winter storm warning is issued, it's time to put your preparation into action and make safety your top priority.

### If You're at Home

Stay indoors and avoid unnecessary travel. If you must go outside, dress in layers with waterproof outer garments. Keep updated on weather conditions through local news or weather radio. Check on elderly neighbors or those who may need assistance, but prioritize your own safety first.

During extended power outages, conserve heat by closing off unused rooms and covering windows at night. Never use generators, grills, or camp stoves indoors, as they produce deadly carbon monoxide. If you're running low on critical supplies or experiencing a medical emergency during a storm, **One Tap Alert's instant SOS button** lets you alert all your emergency contacts simultaneously with a simple press-and-hold for just one second, even if phone lines are down or cell service is limited.

### If You're Caught Outdoors or in Your Car

If you're stranded in your vehicle, stay with your car unless help is visible within 100 yards. Run the engine and heater for about 10 minutes each hour, ensuring the exhaust pipe is clear of snow. Tie a brightly colored cloth to your antenna or window to signal for help.

If you must travel during winter weather, advance planning and constant communication are essential.

## Post-Storm Safety Considerations

After the storm passes, new dangers emerge. Downed power lines, unstable snow loads on roofs, and carbon monoxide from generators all pose serious risks.

Snow removal is one of the most dangerous post-storm activities, with thousands of heart attacks and injuries occurring annually. If you must shovel, take frequent breaks and lift with your legs, not your back. Before beginning extended outdoor work in cold conditions, set a **Safety Timer on One Tap Alert**. Walk carefully around your property, watching for ice, unstable snow, and hidden hazards.
