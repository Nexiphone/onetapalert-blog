## Understanding the Risks of Running Alone

Before diving into solutions, it's important to understand what makes solo running potentially risky. Awareness of these challenges is the first step toward mitigating them.

### Physical Risks

Solo runners face the possibility of injuries without immediate help nearby. A twisted ankle, sudden cramp, or more serious injury like a fall can leave you stranded far from assistance. Medical emergencies such as cardiac events, dehydration, or heat stroke are also more dangerous when you're alone.

### Navigation and Environmental Risks

Getting lost on unfamiliar trails, encountering aggressive wildlife, or facing sudden weather changes are all concerns for solo runners. Without a companion to help problem-solve or share resources, these situations become more challenging.

### Personal Safety Concerns

Unfortunately, solo runners—particularly women—face the reality of potential harassment or assault. According to various running safety surveys, a significant percentage of runners have experienced harassment, catcalling, or felt unsafe during their runs. While these incidents shouldn't prevent anyone from running alone, they do require proactive safety measures.

## Pre-Run Safety Planning

The foundation of safe solo running starts before you even lace up your shoes. Proper planning can prevent many dangerous situations and ensure help can reach you quickly if needed.

### Share Your Running Plans

Always tell someone where you're running and when you expect to return. This simple step ensures someone will notice if you don't come back as planned. With One Tap Alert's safety timer feature, you can automate this process—set a countdown for your expected run duration, and if you don't check in when you return, your emergency contacts are automatically alerted with your last known location.

### Choose Your Route Wisely

Stick to well-populated, well-lit areas, especially when running early in the morning or after dark. Familiarize yourself with your route before running it alone, noting landmarks, businesses, and areas with reliable cell service. Avoid isolated trails or areas with poor visibility until you're comfortable with the terrain.

### Time Your Runs Strategically

If possible, run during daylight hours when more people are around. If you must run in the dark, choose routes with good street lighting and consider wearing reflective gear. Early morning and evening runs present different safety considerations—morning routes may be darker but have commuter traffic, while evening routes might be lighter but potentially more isolated.

## Safety Strategies During Your Run

Your awareness and behavior during your run are just as important as your planning beforehand.

### Stay Alert and Aware

This is perhaps the most important running safety tip: stay aware of your surroundings. If you listen to music or podcasts, keep the volume low enough to hear approaching vehicles, cyclists, or people. Consider using only one earbud or bone-conduction headphones that don't block ambient noise.

### Trust Your Instincts

If something feels wrong, it probably is. Don't hesitate to change your route, cross the street, or head toward a populated area if you feel uncomfortable. Your gut instinct is a powerful safety tool—never ignore it to avoid seeming rude or paranoid.

### Vary Your Routine

Running the same route at the same time every day makes you predictable. While routines are comfortable, varying your routes and times makes it harder for anyone with ill intentions to anticipate your whereabouts.

### Run Against Traffic

When running on roads, always run against the direction of traffic so you can see oncoming vehicles. This gives you maximum time to react if a driver doesn't see you or a vehicle crosses into your path.

### Keep Someone Updated on Long Runs

For longer runs, especially on trails or in remote areas, real-time location sharing provides peace of mind. ## What to Do If You Feel Unsafe While Running

Despite all precautions, you may find yourself in an uncomfortable or dangerous situation. Here's how to respond.

### Head Toward People and Businesses

If you feel threatened, immediately move toward populated areas, open businesses, or well-traveled streets. Duck into a store, restaurant, or gas station if needed. Most people are willing to help if you explain you feel unsafe.

### Use Your Safety App Immediately

Don't wait until a situation escalates—if you feel threatened, activate One Tap Alert's SOS button immediately. Your emergency contacts will receive your location and can call authorities or come to your location. The app works even if you keep running, ensuring your contacts can track your movement in real-time.

### Make Noise and Draw Attention

If someone is following or harassing you, don't stay quiet. Shout loudly, make a scene, and draw attention from others nearby. Most predators rely on victims staying quiet—making noise often deters them.

### Call 911 When Necessary

If you're in immediate danger, calling 911 should be your priority. However, in situations where you can't safely make a phone call or aren't sure if you need police intervention yet, activating your safety app alerts your personal network who can assess the situation and call authorities if needed.

## Safety Tips for Different Running Environments

Different running environments present unique challenges that require specific strategies.

### Urban and Neighborhood Running

Stay on well-lit streets, run against traffic, and be extra cautious at intersections and driveways. Urban environments offer the advantage of people and businesses nearby but require heightened awareness of traffic and multiple directions of approach.

### Trail and Nature Running

Trail running offers beauty and solitude but comes with added risks. Always tell someone your specific trail and expected return time—this is where One Tap Alert's safety timer becomes essential. Carry extra water, a basic first aid kit, and know that cell service may be spotty. Download offline maps before heading into remote areas.

### Track Running

Track running is generally safer due to being in a contained, often supervised area, but it's still wise to remain aware of who else is at the track, especially during early morning or late evening sessions when fewer people are around.
