## How Much Water Do You Actually Need?

The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) recommends storing at least one gallon of water per person per day for a minimum of three days. However, this is a bare minimum baseline.

### Breaking Down Your Water Needs

For a more realistic emergency water supply calculation, consider:

**Drinking and Hydration:** 0.5-1 gallon per person per day (more in hot climates or for active individuals)

**Food Preparation:** 0.25 gallons per person per day

**Hygiene and Sanitation:** 0.25-0.5 gallons per person per day

**Total Recommended:** 1-2 gallons per person per day

For a family of four planning for a 72-hour emergency, that's 12-24 gallons of water. A two-week supply—which preparedness experts increasingly recommend—requires 56-112 gallons.

Don't forget to account for:
- Pets (approximately 1 ounce per pound of body weight daily)
- Infants (who may need extra water for formula)
- Medications that require water
- Special medical needs

## Best Containers for Emergency Water Storage

Not all containers are created equal when it comes to water storage. Your choice of container directly impacts water safety and shelf life.

### Food-Grade Water Storage Containers

The gold standard for emergency water storage is food-grade plastic containers specifically designed for water. Look for containers marked with recycling codes 1, 2, 4, or 7, and ensure they're BPA-free.

**Recommended options:**
- 5-gallon BPA-free water jugs (stackable and portable)
- 55-gallon water barrels (for long-term home storage)
- WaterBrick containers (modular, stackable, and dual-purpose)
- Military-style water cans (extremely durable)

**Avoid:**
- Milk jugs (degrade quickly and can't be properly sanitized)
- Containers that previously held non-food items
- Glass containers (breakage risk during disasters)

### Commercial Bottled Water

Store-bought bottled water is convenient and has a long shelf life when stored properly. Advantages include:
- Already purified and sealed
- Portable and easy to distribute
- Clear expiration dates
- No preparation required

Purchase cases of bottled water and rotate them before expiration dates. While slightly more expensive per gallon than DIY storage, bottled water eliminates preparation time and reduces contamination risk.

## How to Store Water Safely for Long-Term Emergencies

Proper storage techniques ensure your emergency water supply remains safe to drink for months or years.

### Step-by-Step Water Storage Process

**1. Choose the Right Location**

Store water in a cool, dark place away from:
- Direct sunlight (promotes algae growth)
- Extreme temperatures (basements are ideal)
- Concrete floors (place on wooden pallets or shelving)
- Chemicals, gasoline, or pesticides (plastic can absorb fumes)

**2. Sanitize Containers**

Before filling, wash containers with dish soap and water, then sanitize with a bleach solution (1 teaspoon unscented bleach per quart of water). Rinse thoroughly.

**3. Fill with Clean Water**

Use tap water from a treated municipal source when possible. Fill containers to the top to minimize air exposure, then seal tightly.

**4. Label and Date**

Mark each container with the storage date using permanent marker. This helps you rotate supplies and maintain freshness.

**5. Rotate Your Supply**

Replace stored water every 6-12 months. Use old water for gardening or cleaning, then refill with fresh water.

### Should You Add Preservatives?

If you're using treated municipal water, no additional treatment is necessary for 6-12 month storage. For longer-term storage or well water, consider:

- **Household bleach:** Add 1/8 teaspoon (8 drops) of unscented liquid chlorine bleach per gallon of water
- **Water purification tablets:** Follow manufacturer instructions
- **Commercial water preservatives:** Designed specifically for long-term storage

## Alternative Emergency Water Sources

When your primary water supply runs out, knowing how to identify and access alternative sources can be lifesaving.

### Safe Alternative Sources (with Treatment)

**Water Heaters:** Your home water heater holds 30-50 gallons of water. Turn off gas/electricity, let cool, then drain from the bottom valve. Filter and purify before drinking.

**Toilet Tanks:** The tank (not bowl) contains several gallons of clean water—if you don't use chemical toilet cleaners. This water should be purified before consumption.

**Pipes:** After shutting off the main water valve to prevent contaminated water from entering, you can drain the pipes by opening the highest faucet and collecting water from the lowest.

**Rainwater:** Collect rain in clean containers. Always purify before drinking, as roofing materials and atmospheric contaminants can affect safety.

**Natural Sources:** Streams, rivers, lakes, and ponds can provide water in emergencies, but require thorough purification. Choose moving water over stagnant when possible.

### Sources to Avoid

Never drink from:
- Radiators or water beds (contain chemicals)
- Swimming pools treated with chemicals (for drinking)
- Floodwater (contaminated with sewage and chemicals)
- Water with floating material, dark color, or odor

## Water Purification Methods for Emergencies

Even water that looks clean can harbor bacteria, viruses, and parasites. During emergencies, assume all water from alternative sources needs purification.

### Boiling: The Most Reliable Method

Boiling kills virtually all pathogens and is the gold standard for water purification.

**Process:**
1. Filter water through clean cloth to remove particles
2. Bring to a rolling boil
3. Boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 feet elevation)
4. Let cool and store in clean containers

Boiling requires fuel, so stock up on camping stoves, propane, or other emergency cooking methods.

### Chemical Treatment

**Bleach Purification:**
- Add 1/8 teaspoon (8 drops) of unscented 6% household bleach per gallon of clear water
- Double the amount for cloudy water
- Mix well and let stand 30 minutes
- Water should have a slight chlorine smell; if not, repeat and wait another 15 minutes

**Water Purification Tablets:**
- Convenient and portable
- Follow package directions (usually 30 minutes to 4 hours)
- Effective against most bacteria and viruses
- Some don't kill Cryptosporidium (combine with filtration)

### Water Filters and Purifiers

Portable water filters are essential emergency supplies:

**Gravity Filters:** Large capacity, perfect for families (Platypus GravityWorks, LifeStraw Mission)

**Pump Filters:** Reliable and fast (Katadyn Hiker Pro, MSR Guardian)

**Bottle Filters:** Portable and individual (LifeStraw, Sawyer Mini)

**UV Purifiers:** Fast and effective against viruses (SteriPEN)

Stock multiple purification methods as backups. Different situations may require different approaches.

## Common Emergency Water Mistakes to Avoid

Learn from others' mistakes to keep your water supply safe and effective.

### Storage Mistakes

**Storing in wrong containers:** Never use containers that held milk, juice, or chemicals. These can't be adequately sanitized and will contaminate your water.

**Ignoring temperature:** Water stored in hot garages or attics can develop bacterial growth and leach chemicals from plastic containers.

**Forgetting to rotate:** Water doesn't last forever. Set calendar reminders to refresh supplies every 6-12 months.

**Poor labeling:** Always date containers. During emergencies, you won't remember which water was stored when.

### Purification Mistakes

**Not filtering before purifying:** Sediment and particles protect pathogens from chemicals and UV light. Always filter cloudy water first.

**Insufficient contact time:** Chemical treatments need full exposure time. Don't rush the process.

**Using scented bleach:** Only use unscented bleach for water purification. Additives can be harmful.

**Assuming clear water is safe:** Clear water can still contain dangerous pathogens. Always purify water from unknown sources.

### Emergency Response Mistakes

**Hoarding water during crises:** Taking more than you need during emergencies hurts your community and creates dangerous scarcity.

**Not communicating your status:** Keep emergency contacts updated using **One Tap Alert** so they know you're safe and whether you need water assistance.

**Venturing out alone:** Always use the **Safety Timer** or share your location when leaving for water supplies during emergencies.

**Drinking contaminated water out of desperation:** Most waterborne illnesses take days to develop. Purify first, even if very thirsty.
