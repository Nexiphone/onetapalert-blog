## Pre-Trip Health and Medical Preparation

Before embarking on any journey, seniors should prioritize health preparation to ensure a safe and enjoyable trip.

### Schedule a Pre-Travel Medical Checkup

Visit your doctor at least 4-6 weeks before departure. This appointment should cover:

- A general health assessment to ensure you're fit for travel
- Review of all current medications and prescriptions
- Discussion of any necessary vaccinations for your destination
- Recommendations for managing chronic conditions while traveling
- A written summary of your medical history

Ask your doctor to provide a letter detailing your medical conditions, medications, and any special needs. Store this document in **One Tap Alert's Secure Vault**, where you can access it instantly from your iPhone, even without internet connection. This end-to-end encrypted storage ensures your sensitive medical information is always at your fingertips but never compromised.

### Pack a Comprehensive Medical Kit

Create a travel medical kit that includes:

- All prescription medications in their original containers (pack extra in case of delays)
- Over-the-counter medications (pain relievers, antacids, anti-diarrheal medication)
- First aid supplies (bandages, antiseptic wipes, thermometer)
- Copies of all prescriptions with generic drug names
- Medical alert bracelet or necklace if you have specific conditions
- List of emergency contacts and your doctor's contact information

Having digital copies of prescriptions and medical documents stored securely means you won't panic if your physical copies are lost. The Secure Vault feature also lets you store insurance cards, making it easy to access policy numbers when needed abroad.

### Research Healthcare Options at Your Destination

Before you leave:

- Identify hospitals and medical facilities near your accommodation
- Understand what your health insurance covers internationally
- Consider purchasing travel medical insurance for comprehensive coverage
- Learn basic medical phrases in the local language
- Save emergency numbers for your destination country

### Establish a Communication Schedule

Create a regular check-in routine with family members or friends back home. This might include:

- Daily text messages or quick calls at predetermined times
- Sharing your itinerary before departure
- Providing hotel names and contact information
- Updating loved ones about any changes to your plans

## Practical Safety Tips for Seniors Traveling Abroad

Beyond technology, implement these practical strategies to maximize your safety while traveling.

### Choose Accommodations Wisely

Select hotels and accommodations with senior-friendly features:

- Ground floor rooms or reliable elevators
- Well-lit hallways and entrances
- 24-hour front desk service
- Located in safe, well-traveled neighborhoods
- Good reviews from other senior travelers
- Accessibility features if you have mobility concerns

Share your accommodation details with emergency contacts and save the address in **One Tap Alert's** notes section for quick reference.

### Be Smart About Money and Valuables

Protect yourself from theft and financial loss:

- Use hotel safes for passports, extra cash, and valuable jewelry
- Carry only the credit cards and cash you need for the day
- Keep digital copies of credit cards in your Secure Vault
- Use a money belt or hidden travel pouch
- Avoid displaying expensive jewelry, cameras, or electronics
- Notify your bank and credit card companies about travel plans

If you're robbed, having all your important document information securely stored means you can quickly contact banks, report your passport missing to the embassy, and provide necessary information to local authorities—all without the physical documents.

### Navigate Transportation Safely

Getting around safely is crucial for senior travelers:

- Use official taxis or reputable rideshare services
- Before getting in a vehicle, activate the Safety Timer with your expected arrival time
- Sit in the back seat of taxis
- Keep your belongings close and visible
- Avoid traveling alone late at night in unfamiliar areas
- Consider guided tours for complicated destinations

The real-time location sharing capability means that if you feel uncomfortable during a taxi ride or any transportation situation, activating the SOS button immediately lets your contacts see your location and track your movement.

### Stay Aware of Your Surroundings

Maintain situational awareness to avoid potentially dangerous situations:

- Stay in well-populated, well-lit areas, especially at night
- Keep your phone charged and easily accessible
- Trust your instincts—if something feels wrong, remove yourself from the situation
- Be cautious of strangers offering unsolicited help
- Keep your hotel room number private
- Avoid discussing travel plans or accommodations with strangers

### Manage Medications While Traveling

Keeping up with medications across time zones requires planning:

- Set alarms for medication times adjusted to new time zones
- Keep medications in carry-on luggage, never checked bags
- Bring more medication than you think you'll need
- Store pharmacy contact information in your Secure Vault
- Keep a list of generic drug names in case you need refills abroad
