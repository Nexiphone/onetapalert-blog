## Pre-Trip Vehicle Preparation

Your vehicle is your lifeline on a road trip, so proper maintenance isn't optional—it's essential.

### Complete a Full Vehicle Inspection

Schedule a professional inspection at least one week before departure. A mechanic should check your brakes, battery, alternator, belts, hoses, and fluid levels. Don't skip this step even if your car "seems fine." Many breakdowns happen because of issues that weren't obvious to the driver.

Pay special attention to your tires. Check the tread depth using the penny test (insert a penny into the tread with Lincoln's head upside down—if you can see all of his head, replace the tire). Verify that all tires, including your spare, are inflated to the manufacturer's recommended PSI.

### Pack an Emergency Kit

Your emergency kit should include:
- Jumper cables or a portable jump starter
- Spare tire, jack, and lug wrench
- Basic tool set
- Flashlight with extra batteries
- First aid kit
- Reflective triangles or flares
- Fire extinguisher
- Duct tape and zip ties
- Bottled water and non-perishable snacks

## Safe Driving Practices

### Combat Driver Fatigue

Drowsy driving causes an estimated 100,000 crashes annually in the United States. Combat fatigue by:
- Taking a 15-20 minute break every two hours
- Switching drivers regularly if possible
- Avoiding heavy meals that make you sleepy
- Never driving between midnight and 6 AM if you're normally asleep during those hours
- Pulling over immediately if you yawn repeatedly, have trouble keeping your eyes open, or drift between lanes

### Minimize Distractions

Put your phone in Do Not Disturb mode while driving. If you need navigation, mount your phone at eye level and set the route before you start moving. Never text, take photos, or scroll through apps while the vehicle is in motion.

If traveling with passengers, designate a "co-pilot" to handle navigation, music, and communication. This person can also help watch for hazards and keep the driver alert.

### Adjust for Weather Conditions

Check the forecast for your entire route, not just your destination. If severe weather is predicted:
- Delay your departure if possible
- Add extra travel time to your estimate
- Reduce speed and increase following distance
- Turn on headlights even during daytime
- Avoid using cruise control in rain or snow

## Accommodation Safety

### Research Before Booking

Read recent reviews focusing specifically on safety and location. Look for mentions of:
- Well-lit parking areas
- Security cameras or on-site security
- Electronic key cards (more secure than physical keys)
- Neighborhood safety

When possible, book accommodations on the second or third floor—high enough to deter break-ins through windows, but low enough for emergency evacuation if needed.

### Practice Hotel Safety

Once you check in:
- Verify that all locks and security features work before unpacking
- Use the deadbolt and security chain whenever you're in the room
- Don't answer the door without verifying who's there
- Keep valuables out of sight from windows
- Know the location of emergency exits

If you're staying somewhere unfamiliar, use One Tap Alert's Safety Timer when you're settling in for the night. This is especially useful for solo travelers—set it for your sleep duration, and if an emergency prevents you from checking in when you wake up, your contacts will be notified.

## Solo Road Trip Considerations

Traveling alone offers incredible freedom but requires additional safety precautions.

### Maintain Communication

Establish a regular check-in schedule with someone back home—perhaps a quick text every evening. If you miss a check-in, they know to follow up.

With One Tap Alert, you can share your location with unlimited emergency contacts, so multiple trusted people can keep tabs on your journey. This redundancy ensures someone will notice if something's wrong, even if your primary contact is unavailable.

### Trust Your Instincts

If a rest stop, gas station, or area gives you uncomfortable feelings, trust that instinct and move on. Your safety is more important than sticking to a schedule or finding the cheapest gas.

Park in well-lit areas near building entrances. When returning to your vehicle, have your keys ready and check the back seat before getting in.

### Be Discreet About Solo Status

At hotels or when meeting new people, you don't need to advertise that you're traveling alone. Use phrases like "we're planning to..." or mention joining up with friends the next day. Keep your room number private and avoid specific details about your plans with strangers.

## Rest Stop and Gas Station Safety

### Choose Your Stops Wisely

Select rest stops and gas stations that are:
- Well-lit and busy
- Visible from the road
- Associated with major brands (which typically have better security)
- Recommended by recent reviews or travel apps

Avoid stopping at isolated locations, especially after dark.

### Stay Alert

While at rest stops:
- Keep your vehicle locked even when you're just stepping away momentarily
- Don't leave valuables visible in your car
- Stay aware of your surroundings—avoid staring at your phone
- Park in busy areas near the main building
- Have your keys ready before walking to your car

If someone approaches you and makes you uncomfortable, walk toward populated areas or the main building. The SOS button in One Tap Alert works anywhere you have cell service, allowing you to call for help discreetly if you feel threatened.

## Food and Hydration Safety

### Stay Properly Hydrated

Dehydration impairs cognitive function and reaction time—both critical for safe driving. Keep plenty of water in the car and drink regularly, not just when you feel thirsty.

However, balance hydration with bathroom breaks. Plan stops at safe, public facilities rather than emergency roadside stops in isolated areas.

### Choose Food Carefully

Avoid heavy, greasy meals that can make you drowsy. Opt for:
- Protein-rich snacks (nuts, jerky, hard-boiled eggs)
- Fresh fruits and vegetables
- Whole grain crackers or bars
- Light sandwiches

Pack a cooler with healthy options to avoid relying entirely on fast food and convenience stores.

## Special Considerations for Different Road Trip Types

### National Park Road Trips

National parks present unique safety challenges with remote locations, wildlife, and variable weather. Always:
- Check park alerts and road closures before entering
- Carry physical maps as backup to digital navigation
- Understand wildlife safety protocols specific to the park
- Tell a park ranger your planned route and return time for backcountry activities

The Safety Timer feature in One Tap Alert is particularly valuable for park activities like hiking or wildlife viewing, where you might be away from your vehicle for hours in areas with limited cell service.

### Urban Road Trips

City driving requires different safety considerations:
- Research parking in advance; choose secure lots over street parking
- Be extra vigilant for pedestrians, cyclists, and aggressive drivers
- Don't leave GPS devices, phone mounts, or other tech visible in parked cars
- Avoid driving in unfamiliar cities during rush hour when possible

### Coastal Road Trips

Coastal routes offer stunning views but can present hazards like:
- Sudden fog that reduces visibility to nearly zero
- Roads damaged by erosion or flooding
- Limited cell service in remote coastal areas
- Extreme weather changes

Monitor weather closely and be prepared to change plans if conditions deteriorate.

## What to Do in an Emergency

### If You Break Down

1. Pull as far off the road as safely possible
2. Turn on hazard lights
3. If it's safe to exit the vehicle and you're on a highway, exit on the passenger side away from traffic
4. Set up reflective triangles or flares if you have them
5. Use One Tap Alert's SOS button to notify your emergency contacts of your exact location
6. Call roadside assistance
7. Stay with your vehicle unless it's in immediate danger
8. If you must leave the vehicle, leave a note on the dashboard with your contact information and direction traveled

### If You're in an Accident

1. Check for injuries and call 911 if anyone is hurt
2. Move vehicles out of traffic if possible and safe
3. Turn on hazard lights
4. Exchange information with other drivers (names, contact info, insurance details, license plate numbers)
5. Document the scene with photos
6. Use One Tap Alert to notify your emergency contacts—they can help coordinate additional support, contact your insurance company, or arrange alternative transportation
7. File a police report, especially if there are injuries or significant damage

### If You Feel Unsafe

Trust your instincts. If you feel threatened at a rest stop, gas station, or anywhere else:
1. Get back in your vehicle and lock the doors
2. Drive to a police station or well-lit, populated area
3. Use the SOS button in One Tap Alert to notify your emergency contacts
4. Call 911 if you're in immediate danger
5. Don't worry about overreacting—your safety is paramount
