## Understanding Why Seniors Fall

Before implementing prevention strategies, it's important to understand the common causes of falls in older adults:

- **Physical changes**: Decreased muscle strength, reduced flexibility, and balance issues
- **Medications**: Certain prescriptions can cause dizziness or drowsiness
- **Vision problems**: Cataracts, glaucoma, and reduced depth perception
- **Environmental hazards**: Poor lighting, loose rugs, and cluttered walkways
- **Chronic conditions**: Arthritis, Parkinson's disease, and stroke can affect mobility
- **Footwear**: Improper shoes or worn-out slippers increase fall risk

## Home Safety Modifications to Prevent Falls

### Remove Tripping Hazards

The majority of senior falls happen at home, making environmental modifications crucial:

- Remove throw rugs or secure them with non-slip backing
- Keep electrical cords along walls and out of walkways
- Eliminate clutter from floors, stairs, and hallways
- Store items at accessible heights to avoid reaching or bending
- Repair loose floorboards or uneven surfaces immediately

After making these modifications, consider setting up the **Safety Timer feature in One Tap Alert** when moving through your home. ### Improve Lighting Throughout the Home

Poor visibility is a major contributor to falls, especially at night:

- Install bright LED bulbs in all rooms, hallways, and stairwells
- Add motion-sensor nightlights in bedrooms, bathrooms, and hallways
- Keep flashlights or rechargeable lights within easy reach of the bed
- Ensure light switches are accessible at room entrances
- Consider smart lighting that can be voice-activated

Even with perfect lighting, accidents can still happen. ### Install Bathroom Safety Features

Bathrooms are particularly hazardous due to wet, slippery surfaces:

- Install grab bars near toilets and inside showers/tubs
- Use non-slip mats both inside and outside the bathtub or shower
- Consider a walk-in shower or bath transfer bench
- Add a raised toilet seat with armrests if needed
- Keep towels, soap, and toiletries within easy reach

Many seniors worry about showering alone. The **Safety Timer in One Tap Alert** is perfect for this scenario—set a 15-minute timer before stepping into the shower, and if you don't check in afterward, your trusted contacts will know something may be wrong and can check on you immediately.

### Secure Staircases and Railings

Stairs present one of the highest fall risks in multi-level homes:

- Install sturdy handrails on both sides of all staircases
- Ensure railings extend beyond the top and bottom steps
- Add non-slip tread strips or carpet to each step
- Mark the edge of steps with contrasting tape for better visibility
- Keep stairways clear of objects at all times
- Ensure adequate lighting at the top and bottom of stairs

## Physical Strength and Balance Strategies

### Exercise Regularly for Strength and Balance

Physical fitness is one of the most effective fall prevention strategies:

- **Balance exercises**: Tai chi, yoga, and standing on one foot
- **Strength training**: Light weights or resistance bands twice weekly
- **Walking programs**: 30 minutes of walking most days
- **Flexibility exercises**: Stretching to maintain range of motion

When starting a new exercise routine, especially outdoor walks, use the **Safety Timer feature in One Tap Alert**. ### Review Medications with Healthcare Providers

Some medications increase fall risk through side effects like dizziness, drowsiness, or low blood pressure:

- Schedule a comprehensive medication review with your doctor or pharmacist
- Discuss alternatives that may have fewer side effects
- Never stop medications without professional guidance
- Understand how medications interact with each other
- Be aware of when medications peak in your system

Keep copies of your medication lists in the **Secure Vault feature of One Tap Alert**, where you can store medical information with end-to-end encryption. If you fall and responders arrive, your emergency contacts or medical professionals can quickly access your critical health information.

### Get Regular Vision and Hearing Checkups

Sensory impairments significantly increase fall risk:

- Have annual eye examinations and update prescriptions
- Address cataracts or glaucoma promptly
- Get hearing tested and use hearing aids if recommended
- Clean eyeglasses daily for optimal clarity
- Use proper lighting when reading or doing detailed tasks

Store copies of your vision prescriptions, hearing test results, and insurance information in **One Tap Alert's Secure Vault** so everything is accessible during emergencies.

## Footwear and Mobility Aids

### Choose Appropriate Footwear

Proper shoes provide stability and traction:

- Wear sturdy, low-heeled shoes with non-slip soles
- Avoid walking in socks, stockings, or floppy slippers
- Ensure shoes fit properly and provide good support
- Replace worn-out shoes with smooth treads
- Consider shoes with Velcro closures for easier fastening

### Use Mobility Aids Correctly

Assistive devices can prevent falls when used properly:

- Get fitted for canes or walkers by a physical therapist
- Ensure mobility aids are the correct height
- Check rubber tips regularly and replace when worn
- Use mobility aids consistently, not just "when needed"
- Consider a rollator walker with a seat for resting during walks

When using mobility aids outside the home—whether walking to the mailbox or going to the store—activate the **Safety Timer in One Tap Alert**. This gives you confidence knowing that if you lose your balance or become too fatigued to return home safely, help will be on the way.
