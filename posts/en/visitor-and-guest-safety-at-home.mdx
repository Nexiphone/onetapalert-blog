## Understanding Your Responsibilities as a Host

When you invite someone into your home, you take on a responsibility for their wellbeing. This doesn't mean you're legally liable for every possible accident, but it does mean that thoughtful preparation can prevent most incidents before they happen.

Your guests may not be familiar with the layout of your home, the quirks of your property, or the local area. They're relying on you to provide a safe environment and clear communication about any potential hazards. This is especially important for elderly visitors, children, or guests with mobility issues who may face additional risks.

Beyond physical safety, emotional security matters too. Guests should feel comfortable reaching out for help if they need it, and they should know what to do in case of an emergency. ## Pre-Arrival Safety Preparations

### Conduct a Home Safety Audit

Before guests arrive, walk through your home with fresh eyes. Look for potential hazards that you've become accustomed to but might pose risks to unfamiliar visitors:

- **Tripping hazards**: Loose rugs, electrical cords, uneven flooring, or clutter in walkways
- **Lighting issues**: Dark stairwells, dim hallways, or burned-out bulbs
- **Bathroom safety**: Slippery tubs or showers without non-slip mats
- **Fire hazards**: Overloaded outlets, blocked exits, or malfunctioning smoke detectors
- **Outdoor dangers**: Uneven pavement, icy walkways, or poorly maintained steps

Fix what you can before your guests arrive, and make mental notes of things you'll need to point out during their stay.

### Prepare Emergency Information

Create a simple information sheet for your guests that includes:

- Your home address (guests often don't know the exact address)
- Emergency contact numbers (911, poison control, your cell phone)
- Location of first aid supplies, fire extinguishers, and emergency exits
- Instructions for any security systems or door locks
- Medical facility locations and directions

Consider sharing the **One Tap Alert** app with your guests before they arrive. Once they download it, they can add you as an emergency contact and immediately have access to the SOS button and live location sharing features. This means if they go out exploring your neighborhood or local attractions, you'll be able to check on them if needed.

### Stock Essential Safety Supplies

Make sure you have these items readily available:

- Well-stocked first aid kit
- Working flashlights with fresh batteries
- Fire extinguisher (properly charged and accessible)
- Carbon monoxide and smoke detectors with working batteries
- Emergency numbers posted near phones
- Basic medications (pain relievers, antihistamines, antacids)

## Safety Considerations for Different Types of Guests

### Elderly Visitors

Older guests may have mobility limitations, vision impairments, or medical conditions that require special considerations:

- Install temporary grab bars in bathrooms if needed
- Remove all tripping hazards and secure loose rugs with non-slip pads
- Provide nightlights in hallways, bathrooms, and guest rooms
- Ensure their room is on the ground floor if stairs are difficult
- Keep their medications organized and remind them to take them

The **One Tap Alert** safety timer feature is particularly valuable for elderly guests. If they're taking a walk around the neighborhood or going to bed with medical concerns, they can set a timer that will automatically alert you and other emergency contacts if they don't check in. This provides independence while ensuring someone will respond if there's a problem.

### Children and Teens

Young visitors require different safety measures:

- Childproof cabinets containing cleaning supplies, medications, and sharp objects
- Secure heavy furniture that could tip over
- Cover electrical outlets
- Lock or monitor access to pools, hot tubs, or other water features
- Establish clear house rules about where they can and cannot go

For teenage guests who may want to explore on their own, having them download **One Tap Alert** gives them a direct line to help while giving you peace of mind. The real-time location sharing means you'll know where they are without constantly texting, striking a balance between safety and independence.

### Guests with Disabilities

Accessibility and safety go hand-in-hand for guests with physical or cognitive disabilities:

- Ensure wheelchair accessibility to guest rooms and bathrooms
- Provide clear pathways free of obstacles
- Offer visual and auditory alerts for alarms and doorbells
- Communicate clearly about any potential hazards
- Ask about specific needs before arrival

### Service Providers and Contractors

When workers visit your home, different safety protocols apply:

- Verify their identity and credentials before allowing entry
- Establish clear working areas and communicate which spaces are off-limits
- Secure valuables and sensitive documents (use **One Tap Alert's** secure vault to store digital copies)
- Maintain visibility of work areas
- Set clear start and end times for work

## Emergency Preparedness During the Visit

### Conduct a Safety Orientation

When guests first arrive, give them a brief safety orientation:

- Show them emergency exits and where fire extinguishers are located
- Demonstrate how to operate any unusual locks, alarms, or systems
- Point out the location of first aid supplies
- Explain any quirks about the house (sticky door, finicky lock, etc.)
- Provide the emergency information sheet

During this orientation, suggest they download **One Tap Alert** if they haven't already. Explain that it's a simple way to ensure help is available if needed, and offer to add yourself as one of their emergency contacts. Most guests will appreciate the extra layer of security, especially if they're in an unfamiliar city.

### Establish Communication Protocols

Set expectations about communication during their stay:

- Exchange phone numbers and ensure everyone has everyone's contact information
- Establish a general plan for check-ins (especially for longer stays)
- Clarify whether they should notify you if they're going out or returning late
- Ensure they know how to reach you in an emergency

The beauty of **One Tap Alert** is that it handles emergency communication automatically. Instead of hoping your guest remembers to call you during a crisis, the SOS button ensures you'll be notified immediately with all the information you need to help.

### Create an Emergency Action Plan

Discuss what should happen in various emergency scenarios:

- **Fire**: Meeting spot outside, evacuation routes, when to call 911
- **Medical emergency**: Location of first aid kit, when to use SOS button, nearest hospital
- **Severe weather**: Safe rooms, emergency supplies, alert systems
- **Security concern**: How to activate security system, when to call police, safe rooms

Practice using **One Tap Alert's** SOS button with your guests (without actually triggering alerts). Show them how simple it is—just press and hold for one second—so they'll remember in a high-stress situation.

## Special Considerations for Overnight Guests

### Nighttime Safety

Darkness creates additional risks:

- Provide nightlights in hallways, bathrooms, and stairs
- Show guests where light switches are located
- Keep a flashlight near the guest bed
- Ensure their path to the bathroom is clear and safe
- Set thermostats to comfortable sleeping temperatures

Before bed, remind guests that they can use the **One Tap Alert** SOS button if they need help during the night for any reason—medical emergency, security concern, or even just if they're locked out after stepping outside.
