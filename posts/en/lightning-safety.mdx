## Understanding Lightning and Its Dangers

Lightning is a massive electrical discharge that occurs during thunderstorms, traveling at speeds up to 270,000 mph and reaching temperatures of approximately 30,000 Kelvin—five times hotter than the sun's surface. This intense energy can strike directly, conduct through the ground, or travel through conductive materials like metal fencing or plumbing.

### The 30-30 Rule

The National Weather Service recommends the 30-30 rule for lightning safety:

1. **Count to 30**: When you see lightning, start counting. If you hear thunder before reaching 30, the storm is close enough to strike you—seek shelter immediately.
2. **Wait 30 minutes**: After hearing the last thunder, wait at least 30 minutes before resuming outdoor activities.

Many people make the fatal mistake of thinking they're safe once the rain stops, but lightning can strike up to 10 miles away from the rainfall area—a phenomenon known as a "bolt from the blue."

### Common Lightning Myths Debunked

- **Myth**: Lightning never strikes the same place twice
- **Reality**: Lightning frequently strikes the same location multiple times, especially tall structures
- **Myth**: Rubber tires protect you in a vehicle
- **Reality**: The metal frame provides protection, not the tires
- **Myth**: If it's not raining, you're safe
- **Reality**: Lightning can strike 10+ miles from the storm center

## Lightning Safety Outdoors

Being caught outdoors during a thunderstorm is one of the most dangerous lightning scenarios. Here's how to protect yourself:

### Seek Proper Shelter Immediately

The only truly safe places during a thunderstorm are:

- **Substantial buildings** with wiring and plumbing that can ground the electrical charge
- **Hard-topped vehicles** with the windows rolled up

Avoid small structures like sheds, pavilions, or tents—they offer no lightning protection. ### When No Shelter Is Available

If you're truly caught in the open with no shelter available:

1. **Avoid high ground** and isolated tall objects like trees
2. **Get off elevated areas** like hills, mountain ridges, or rooftops
3. **Spread out** if you're in a group (at least 15 feet apart) to reduce the number of injuries
4. **Assume the lightning crouch**: Squat low with feet together, hands on knees, head tucked. Minimize contact with the ground
5. **Never lie flat** on the ground—this increases your contact with potentially deadly ground current

## Lightning Safety at Home

While being indoors is safer, lightning can still pose risks inside buildings:

### Stay Away From Conductors

During thunderstorms, avoid:

- **Plumbing**: Don't shower, wash dishes, or wash hands
- **Wired electronics**: Stay off corded phones and away from electrical outlets
- **Windows and doors**: Lightning can travel through gaps
- **Concrete floors and walls**: They often contain metal reinforcement
- **Porches and verandas**: These are not protected areas

### Protect Your Electronics

Lightning can surge through electrical systems, damaging computers, televisions, and other devices:

- Install whole-house surge protectors
- Unplug sensitive electronics during storms
- Use point-of-use surge protectors for valuable equipment
- Avoid using wired devices during storms

### Create an Emergency Plan

Every household should have a lightning safety plan that includes:

- Designated safe rooms away from windows and plumbing
- Battery-powered weather radio or smartphone alerts
- Emergency contact information readily accessible
- First aid supplies for potential lightning injuries

## First Aid for Lightning Strike Victims

If someone is struck by lightning, immediate action is critical:

### Lightning Strike Victims Are Safe to Touch

Contrary to popular belief, lightning victims don't carry an electrical charge. You can and should provide immediate assistance.

### Call 911 Immediately

Activate emergency services right away. If you have **One Tap Alert installed**, press and hold the SOS button to simultaneously alert your emergency contacts with your location while someone else calls 911. Having multiple people aware of the emergency and your exact location increases the speed of professional medical response.

### Perform CPR if Needed

Many lightning strike victims experience cardiac arrest. If the person:

- Is not breathing
- Has no pulse
- Is unconscious

Begin CPR immediately and continue until professional help arrives. Lightning strike victims who receive immediate CPR have a significantly higher survival rate.

### Treat for Shock and Burns

- Move the victim to safety only if additional lightning strikes are imminent
- Keep them warm and lying down
- Treat any visible burns with cool water
- Monitor vital signs continuously
