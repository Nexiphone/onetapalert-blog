## Why Parking Garages Can Be Dangerous

Parking garages create the perfect conditions for potential safety risks. Understanding these vulnerabilities is the first step toward protecting yourself.

### Limited Visibility and Blind Spots

Multi-level parking structures are filled with concrete pillars, sharp corners, and poorly lit areas that create numerous blind spots. These architectural features make it difficult to see what's around the next corner or who might be waiting between vehicles.

### Reduced Foot Traffic

Unlike busy streets or shopping centers, parking garages often have minimal foot traffic, especially during off-peak hours. This isolation means fewer witnesses and less opportunity for immediate help if something goes wrong.

### Multiple Entry and Exit Points

Most parking structures have several stairwells, elevators, and vehicle entrances, making it easy for someone to enter unnoticed or quickly leave the scene. This complexity also means you might not be familiar with the fastest exit route in an emergency.

### Enclosed Spaces

The confined nature of parking garages can work against you. Sound doesn't travel well through concrete structures, meaning calls for help might not be heard outside the garage. ## Essential Parking Garage Safety Tips

### Park Strategically

Where you park matters significantly for your safety. Follow these parking strategies to minimize risk:

**Choose well-lit areas:** Always park near overhead lights or in areas with maximum visibility. Even if it means walking a bit further, the added visibility is worth the extra steps.

**Park near entrances or elevators:** Spots closer to main entrances, elevators, or security stations typically have more foot traffic and better lighting.

**Avoid isolated corners:** Stay away from the top floors or remote corners of parking structures where you'd be alone and far from help.

**Back into spaces:** Parking in reverse allows for a quicker exit and better visibility when you return to your vehicle. You can see your surroundings better and drive away faster if needed.

Before leaving your vehicle, take a moment to set up a **One Tap Alert** safety timer. If you're heading into a shopping mall or appointment for a specific duration, the safety timer will automatically alert your emergency contacts if you don't check back in—providing a safety net even when you're focused on other things.

### Stay Aware of Your Surroundings

Situational awareness is your first line of defense in any environment, but it's especially critical in parking garages.

**Put your phone away:** While walking to or from your car, keep your phone in your pocket or bag. Being absorbed in your screen makes you an easy target and prevents you from noticing potential threats.

**Remove headphones:** Keep your ears free to hear footsteps, voices, or vehicles approaching. Your hearing is a crucial early warning system.

**Scan your environment:** Constantly look around you—behind, beside, and ahead. Notice who else is in the garage and trust your instincts if something feels off.

**Check under and inside your car:** Before getting in, glance underneath your vehicle and check the back seat. This simple habit takes seconds but could prevent a dangerous situation.

If you ever feel uncomfortable, open **One Tap Alert** and keep your finger ready on the SOS button. The app requires only a one-second press and hold to send an instant alert with your live GPS location to all your emergency contacts.

### Have Your Keys Ready

Fumbling for keys while standing next to your car wastes precious time and distracts you from your surroundings.

**Prepare before leaving:** Have your keys in hand before you exit the building or elevator, not when you reach your car.

**Use keyless entry from a distance:** If your vehicle has remote unlocking, use it while you're still several feet away so you can enter immediately.

**Keep keys as a defensive tool:** Hold keys between your fingers if needed, though your primary focus should be on awareness and escape rather than confrontation.

### Travel in Groups When Possible

There's safety in numbers. Whenever you can, coordinate with friends, coworkers, or family to walk to the parking garage together.

**Shop or work together:** If you're at a mall with friends or leaving work with colleagues, make a point to leave as a group.

**Ask security for escorts:** Many facilities offer security escort services, especially during evening hours. Don't hesitate to use this free service.

**Wait for others:** If you're alone and see other people heading toward the garage, consider waiting and walking near them (while maintaining appropriate distance).

### Trust Your Instincts

Your intuition evolved over thousands of years to keep you safe. If something feels wrong, it probably is.

**Don't ignore gut feelings:** If you feel uncomfortable about a person, location, or situation, act on that feeling immediately.

**Change your plans:** If someone is following you or making you uncomfortable, don't go to your car. Instead, return to a populated area, seek security, or call the police.

**Make noise:** If you feel threatened, don't be afraid to yell, scream, or set off your car alarm. Draw attention to yourself.
