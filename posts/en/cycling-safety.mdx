## Pre-Ride Safety Checklist

Before you even leave your driveway, a proper pre-ride safety check can prevent mechanical failures that lead to dangerous situations.

### Bike Inspection

Perform the "ABC Quick Check" before every ride:

- **Air**: Check tire pressure and ensure no visible damage to tubes or tires
- **Brakes**: Test both front and rear brakes for proper function and pad wear
- **Chain**: Ensure proper lubrication and check for rust or stiff links
- **Quick releases**: Verify wheels and seat are properly secured
- **Check**: Overall bike condition, including gears, pedals, and frame integrity

### Essential Safety Gear

Never compromise on these critical items:

- **Helmet**: Replace every 3-5 years or after any impact
- **Lights**: Front white light and rear red light, even for daytime riding
- **Reflective clothing**: High-visibility colors and reflective strips
- **Phone**: Keep it charged and accessible—this is where One Tap Alert becomes your safety lifeline

## Visibility: Your First Line of Defense

Being seen is arguably the most critical factor in cycling safety. The majority of bike-vehicle collisions occur because drivers simply didn't see the cyclist.

### Clothing and Equipment

- **Wear bright, fluorescent colors** during the day (neon yellow, orange, or green)
- **Use reflective gear** for dawn, dusk, and night riding
- **Install multiple lights**: Front (at least 500 lumens), rear (flashing mode increases visibility), and spoke lights
- **Use a helmet-mounted light** to increase your vertical profile and help you be seen from more angles

### Strategic Positioning

- **Take the lane when necessary**: Don't hug the curb on narrow roads where vehicles can't pass safely
- **Make eye contact** with drivers at intersections
- **Use hand signals** clearly and early for all turns and stops
- **Position yourself in drivers' line of sight**, especially at intersections

## Route Planning for Maximum Safety

Strategic route selection can dramatically reduce your exposure to dangerous situations.

### Choosing Your Route

- **Prioritize dedicated bike lanes and trails** whenever possible
- **Use cycling-specific GPS apps** that route you along bike-friendly roads
- **Scout unfamiliar routes** beforehand using street view tools
- **Avoid high-traffic roads during rush hour** when possible
- **Plan routes with cell service** so you can contact help if needed

### Sharing Your Plans

### High-Risk Areas to Navigate Carefully

- **Intersections**: Where 70% of bike-car collisions occur
- **Driveways and parking lot exits**: Drivers may not check for cyclists
- **Right-turn lanes**: Risk of "right hook" accidents
- **Door zones**: Stay at least 4 feet from parked cars to avoid being "doored"

## Road Awareness and Defensive Riding

Defensive riding means constantly anticipating potential hazards and having an escape plan.

### Situational Awareness Techniques

- **Scan ahead 15-20 seconds**: Look far down the road to identify hazards early
- **Check behind regularly**: Use a mirror or turn your head every 10-15 seconds
- **Predict driver behavior**: Assume drivers don't see you
- **Identify escape routes**: Always know where you can bail to avoid a collision
- **Listen for approaching vehicles**: Consider using only one earbud if you listen to music

### Common Hazards to Watch For

- **Road debris**: Glass, gravel, potholes, and storm drain grates
- **Railroad tracks**: Cross at a perpendicular angle when possible
- **Wet surfaces**: Metal plates, painted lines, and leaves become extremely slippery
- **Opening car doors**: The number one urban cycling hazard
- **Distracted drivers**: Increasingly common with smartphone use

## Group Riding Safety

Riding with others requires additional communication and coordination to keep everyone safe.

### Group Riding Etiquette

- **Ride predictably**: Maintain a steady line and avoid sudden movements
- **Communicate hazards**: Point out or call out obstacles, turns, and traffic
- **Maintain safe spacing**: Keep 1-2 bike lengths between riders
- **Single up when necessary**: Form a single-file line on busy roads
- **Stay right, pass left**: Pass only when safe and announce yourself

### Emergency Protocols for Groups

Establish a plan before the ride:
- Designate a group leader and sweep rider
- Agree on hand signals and verbal commands
- Share emergency contact information
- Know the route and bail-out points

## Weather Considerations and Seasonal Safety

Different weather conditions require adapted safety strategies.

### Hot Weather Riding

- **Start hydrated** and drink 20-30 oz per hour
- **Wear moisture-wicking fabrics** to prevent overheating
- **Apply sunscreen** to all exposed skin (reapply every 2 hours)
- **Recognize heat exhaustion signs**: dizziness, nausea, excessive fatigue
- **Ride during cooler hours** when possible

### Cold Weather Riding

- **Layer appropriately**: Base layer, insulation, wind/waterproof outer layer
- **Cover extremities**: Hands, feet, and ears are most vulnerable
- **Watch for ice**: Black ice is invisible and extremely dangerous
- **Adjust tire pressure slightly lower** for better traction
- **Extend braking distance** as stopping takes longer

### Wet Weather Riding

- **Reduce speed by 25-30%**: Everything takes longer in the rain
- **Increase following distance**: Your stopping distance increases significantly
- **Avoid painted lines and metal surfaces**: These become skating rinks when wet
- **Use fenders**: Keep yourself and your bike cleaner and more visible
- **Wear waterproof, reflective gear**: Visibility is even more critical in rain

Extreme weather conditions increase your risk of both accidents and medical emergencies like heat stroke or hypothermia. ## Handling Common Cycling Emergencies

Knowing how to respond when things go wrong can mean the difference between a minor incident and a tragedy.

### If You're Involved in a Collision

1. **Move to safety if possible** (but don't move if you suspect serious injury)
2. **Alert emergency contacts immediately** using One Tap Alert's SOS button
3. **Call 911** for serious injuries or if the driver is fleeing
4. **Document everything**: Photos of the scene, damage, driver's information, witness contacts
5. **Don't admit fault**: Stick to facts when speaking with police and other parties
6. **Seek medical evaluation** even if you feel fine (adrenaline masks injuries)

### If You Witness a Cycling Accident

- **Call 911 immediately** for any significant collision
- **Provide first aid** only if you're trained and it's safe to do so
- **Keep the victim still** if head, neck, or back injury is suspected
- **Stay as a witness** to provide information to police
- **Document the scene** with photos if possible

### Mechanical Failures on the Road

- **Carry basic tools**: Multi-tool, spare tube, tire levers, pump or CO2 cartridges
- **Know basic repairs**: Practice fixing a flat at home before you need to on the road
- **Have a backup plan**: Money for public transit, rideshare apps, or a friend's phone number
- **Know when to call it**: Some mechanical issues aren't roadside-fixable

If you experience a mechanical failure in an isolated area or unsafe location, One Tap Alert can help you coordinate pickup from your emergency contacts by sharing your exact real-time location, ensuring they can find you quickly even if you're in an unfamiliar area.

## Night Riding Safety

Cycling after dark presents unique challenges but can be done safely with proper precautions.

### Lighting Requirements

- **Front light**: Minimum 500 lumens, aimed slightly downward
- **Rear light**: Red, flashing mode, consider multiple rear lights
- **Helmet light**: Increases your visibility and helps you see better
- **Spoke/wheel lights**: Increase your side visibility at intersections
- **Reflective elements**: On frame, wheels, clothing, and helmet

### Night Riding Best Practices

- **Stick to well-lit, familiar routes** when possible
- **Reduce speed by 20-25%**: You can't see hazards as early
- **Be extra cautious at intersections**: Drivers are even less likely to see you
- **Make eye contact with drivers**: Ensure they've seen you before proceeding
- **Ride with others** when possible for increased visibility and safety

Night riding increases your vulnerability significantly. Having One Tap Alert active with the safety timer set means someone is monitoring your expected arrival time and will be alerted if something goes wrong during those higher-risk hours when visibility is limited.
